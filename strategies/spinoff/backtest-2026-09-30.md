# Spin-off backtest: "buy after the forced selling" (run 2026-09-30)

**Strategy file:** `strategies/spinoff/RULES.md` (stage: PAPER)
**Universe:** every US-listed spin-off (SpinCo files with the SEC as a domestic registrant and lists on NYSE/Nasdaq) whose first regular-way trading day fell between 2024-10-01 and 2026-08-31. **n = 22.**
**Data:** SEC EDGAR (Form 10 / 8-K / 10-Q / 10-K, full-text search and submissions API) for the list, dates, ratios and share counts. Yahoo daily bars (adjusted close, so dividends are included) for prices, and SPY adjusted close as the benchmark. Two bars were spot-checked against Massive (HONA 2026-06-29..07-02, NVRI 2026-06-02..05) and matched. Prices run to the **2026-09-29 close**. The 09-30 bar was still intraday, so it was dropped.
**Per-spin CSV:** `strategies/spinoff/backtest-2026-09-30.csv` (source 8-K accession numbers are in the `source` column)

---

## Verdict first

**The rule fails. Retire it as written. Do not paper-trade VYLR under it.**

| Median, net of 3.5% | Buy day-1 close | The RULE (with its stop) | Net minus SPY, same windows |
|---|---|---|---|
| All 22, held to +120d or to date | **+2.5%** (55% positive) | **-11.5%** (9% positive) | day-1: **-5.6%**; rule: **-18.3%** |
| Matched set with a full/resolved 120d for both (n=13) | +3.2% | -10.7% | n/a |
| +60d (complete/resolved) | -5.0% (n=20) | -11.1% (n=21) | day-1: -6.1%; rule: -13.6% |

- **Does the rule beat buying on day 1, on the median, after fees? No.** It does about 14 points worse.
- **Does the rule beat SPY after fees, on the median? No.** It trails by about 18 points over the 120-day window. Only 2 of 22 trades (Q, VGNT) beat SPY.
- **Does buying on day 1 beat SPY after fees, on the median? Also no.** It trails by 2.8 to 6 points depending on the horizon. The median spin in this window did **not** outperform the index once small-account costs are counted.
- The book's own kill rule ("backtest median net of fees ≤ 0 → retire") applies, so the rule is **RETIRED**.

### Why it fails (the mechanism, not bad luck)
1. **The entry fires too early.** "5 days with no new closing low after day 10" fired on **day 11 in 15 of 22 cases** (median entry day 11). The median 40-day post-spin low came on **day 28**, at a median **-14%** below the day-1 close. In 14 of 22 cases the rule bought before the real low. Five quiet days is noise, not exhaustion.
2. **The stop then turns the early entry into a locked-in loss.** The stop sits just below the post-spin low, which at entry was typically only 5 to 9% below the entry price. **18 of 22 trades were stopped out**, a median of about 11 trading days after entry, at a median -8.4% gross (about -12% net).
3. **The stop sold the eventual winners before they worked.**
   - SOLS was stopped the day after entry at -4.7%, then ran +71% (no-stop, 120d).
   - MRP: stopped at -6.8%, then +42%.
   - RHLD: stopped at -11%, then +96%.
   - TRAX: stopped at -17.8%, then +75%.
   - Only Q (+96% net) and VGNT (+40% gross, still open) survived the stop.
4. **Without the stop** it looks better but still doesn't clear SPY on the median. +120d complete (n=13): median net **+6.1%**, net minus SPY **-1.2%**, and only 38% beat SPY. The mean (+15% net) is carried by Q (+100%), RHLD (+96%) and SOLS (+71%). That is exactly the "mean alone doesn't count" trap in the strategy README.
5. **Execution realism makes it slightly worse, not better.** Buying at the next day's open instead of the signal close: median net -11.4%.

## Caveats (read these before trusting any number above)

- **Tiny sample.** 22 spins; only 14 have a full 120-day buy-and-hold window and 13 a complete no-stop rule window. The 2026 spins (TRAX, NVRI, OCTV, FDXF, HONA, MBGL, MFP, ADIG, and VGNT at 120d) are measured to date. One or two names move every median.
- **Regime.**
  - SPY returned **+37.5%** (total return) from 2024-10-01 to 2026-09-29, with one sharp drawdown: -18.8% from 2025-02-19 to 2025-04-08 (the tariff shock). The SNDK and RHLD stop-outs happened inside that drawdown.
  - In a strong bull market a flat-to-slightly-positive spin basket loses to SPY by construction.
  - In a bear market the relative picture could differ, but the stop-out rate would very likely be worse.
- **Look-ahead / hindsight.**
  - The universe was built in 2026 from EDGAR. It includes names that later collapsed (TWNP), so it is not survivor-filtered. But the "US domestic filer" scope and the $2bn size cut were chosen now.
  - Entering at the signal-day close assumes you know the close before it prints (a market-on-close order approximates this). Stops also fill at the close. Both are generous. The next-open variant is shown above.
  - Shares outstanding come from the SpinCo's first 10-Q/10-K cover (weeks after day 1, and after any retained stake), not from day 1 itself.
  - The 3.5% drag is applied once per round trip, flat. Tiny-float names (TWNP, RHLD) probably had wider real spreads.
  - RULES.md entry rules 2 to 4 (business-quality deep dive, volume normalising, written thesis) **cannot be tested mechanically** and were not simulated. They might filter out some losers, but they can't fix the timing/stop problem, which stopped out good businesses (SOLS, MRP) as fast as bad ones.
  - The "max 2 open spin-off positions" cap was not simulated. With it, several 2026 signals would have been skipped.
- **Data quirks.**
  - Yahoo splices when-issued bars, and for NVRI and RNA the old company's history, onto the same ticker. Every series here starts on the regular-way date taken from the 8-K and confirmed by the day-1 volume spike.
  - TWNP's history sits under TWNPQ after delisting.
  - For GCI Liberty, Series C (GLIBK, about 86% of shares) was used, not GLIBA.
- **Special situations inside the sample.**
  - Atrium (RNA) and new Enviri (NVRI) were spun while the parent was being acquired or cashed out, so merger-arb holders were forced sellers.
  - Twin (TWNP) and Resolute (RHLD) had tiny distributed floats: FAT distributed about 0.152 TWNP per FAT share.
  - FedEx kept 19.9% of FDXF; WDC kept 19.9% of SNDK.
  - Amrize and Octave have foreign parents and dual listings.
  - Q joined the S&P 500 on day 1, so there was forced *buying* too.
