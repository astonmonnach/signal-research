# Backtest: December tax-loss selling rebound

Date run: 2026-09-30. Rules under test: `RULES.md` in this folder.
Per-trade rows: `backtest-2026-09-30-trades.csv` (same folder).

## 0. Read this first: the planned test could not be run

The plan was to test formation years 2015 to 2025, which is 11 Decembers. **The data plan only covers 1 of them.**

- The Massive/Polygon key used here returns `HTTP 403 NOT_AUTHORIZED: "Attempted to request data past historical entitlements"` for any date before about 2024-09-30. I checked 2015-01-02, 2021-12-20 and 2024-01-02, and all three were refused. 2024-09-30 was served. That fits a rolling 2-year history limit. The plan is also limited to 5 requests per minute.
- The design needs the close on the first trading day of year Y. The first year where that date is available is **Y = 2025** (start 2025-01-02, exit 2026-01-30). **Formation years 2015 to 2024 are not in this report.**
- As a second, clearly separate data point, I ran a **2024 proxy**. It changes the design: "YTD" is replaced by the return from 2024-09-30 to 2024-12-16 (about 2.5 months), because Jan 2024 prices are not available. The proxy tests whether losers bounce between December and January. It does **not** test the rule as written.

One December, plus one modified December, is not enough to decide whether to trade a seasonal rule. Every number below should be read that way.

## Method

| Item | 2025 (design year) | 2024 (proxy) |
|---|---|---|
| Start price | close 2025-01-02 (first trading day) | close 2024-09-30 (earliest date available) |
| Formation | close 2025-12-15 (Monday) | close 2024-12-16 (15 Dec was a Sunday; Mon 16th is nearest) |
| Entry "Dec 20" | close 2025-12-19 (20 Dec was a Saturday; Fri 19th is 1 day away, Mon 22nd is 2) | close 2024-12-20 |
| Entry "Dec 31" | close 2025-12-31 | close 2024-12-31 |
| Exit | close 2026-01-30 (last trading day of Jan) | close 2025-01-31 |
| Loser rule | YTD <= −40%, or the bottom decile of YTD | Q4 return in the bottom decile (the −40% cut is shown for reference only) |

- **Prices** come from `GET /v2/aggs/grouped/locale/us/market/stocks/{date}`. Returns use `adjusted=true` closes, which are split-adjusted consistently across dates. **Tradability filters use actual (`adjusted=false`) closes** on the entry day: price >= $2 (or >= $5) and close × volume >= $500k. See caveat 4 for why this matters.
- **Universe** is the point-in-time common-stock list from `GET /v3/reference/tickers?type=CS&market=stocks&date=<entry date>`, with all pages fetched (5,243 tickers on 2025-12-19 and 5,164 on 2024-12-20). I used the `date` parameter because it was cheaper than paginating active=true plus active=false. Type CS excludes ETFs, ETNs, funds, warrants, rights, units and ADRs. A stock had to trade on the start, formation and entry days.
- **Benchmark** is the equal-weighted return, from entry to exit, of every stock in that universe passing the same entry-day filters (loser or not). Stocks missing at exit are either excluded or counted as −100%, matching the loser calculation. SPY is also shown.
- **Missing at exit** means the ticker has no bar on the exit date (delisted, acquired or renamed). The reason was not checked. Results are shown both excluding these stocks and treating them as −100%.
- **Fees**: the trader buys positions of about £45 (~$60) at IBKR. A ~$1 minimum commission on each side is ~$2 on $60 (about 3.3%), plus spread. **3.5% is subtracted from every round trip** to get net figures. "% beating fees" is the share of trades with gross return > 3.5%.

What the robustness checks show:
- **The low-liquidity bucket is the worst.** Losers with < $2M entry-day dollar volume lost money gross in both entry variants, with medians of −6.16% and −3.84%. Those are the "small, illiquid losers that big funds avoid" that the thesis relies on, and in this data they did not bounce.
- **A higher price floor helps, but not enough.** Every $5 and $2M cell has a gross median between about 0% and +2%. That is still below the 3.5% fee drag, and at or below the equal-weight universe.
- **Choosing −40% or the bottom decile makes little difference.** In 2025 the decile cut (−37.4%) was close to −40%.
- **No variant, in either year, had a positive net median.** The best was −1.58% (Dec 20, >= $5, >= $2M, 2025).

## 4. Caveats

1. **Sample size: one year (plus one modified proxy year).** This is the main limitation. The January effect is known to vary a lot from year to year, and one December can be driven by a single macro move. December 2025 to January 2026 was a period in which the whole equal-weight universe rose (+2.15% from Dec 19, +3.59% from Dec 31). This backtest cannot say whether 2015 to 2024 behaved differently.
2. **The mean is skewed; use the median.** The clearest case is the 2025 Dec 31 base. The mean was +4.84%, but one trade (BNAI, +966.8%) accounts for most of it. With the top 1% (3 trades) removed, the mean is +0.87% and the median is −0.65%. A trader holding at most 3 positions will almost never hold the one +900% name. The median is the realistic expectation.
3. **Survivorship.** The universe is the point-in-time CS list on the entry date, and the grouped daily bars include names that later delisted. Stocks that disappeared between entry and exit were 5 of 304 (Dec 20) and 3 of 292 (Dec 31) in 2025, and are shown both excluded and at −100%. The −100% treatment is harsh for names that left because they were acquired: CIVI and SMLR may be takeovers, which would be gains, but I did not verify this in the data. Stocks that delisted *between 1 Jan and the formation date* never enter the sample. That is correct for a live rule, since you can only buy what is trading.
4. **Look-ahead in the price filter (found and fixed).** Adjusted grouped bars are adjusted for splits that happened *after* the date. A stock trading at $0.25 in December that later did a 1-for-100 reverse split shows an "adjusted" December close of $25. **255 of the 1,140 candidates** with YTD <= −30% had adjusted ≠ actual close on 2025-12-19 (e.g. SMX: adjusted $39,280, actual $176.03; VMAR: adjusted $1,001.60, actual $0.25). A first pass that filtered on adjusted prices gave the Dec 20 base as N=360, mean −3.19%, median −3.28%. Using actual closes for the filter (the version reported above) removes these sub-$2 names, giving N=304 and mean −0.54%. Returns still use adjusted closes, so reverse splits inside the Dec to Jan window do not create fake returns. Dollar volume (close × volume) is the same whether adjusted or not.
5. **Ticker recycling.** Rows are joined on the ticker string across dates (start, formation, entry, exit). Nothing checks that the ticker is the same company on all dates (no FIGI or CIK join), so a recycled ticker could give a false YTD or exit price. The window is only 13 months and the list is point-in-time at entry, so the risk is small but not zero.
6. **Other look-ahead.** Formation (Dec 15) comes before entry (Dec 19/31), and filters use entry-day data only. One realism gap remains: the backtest buys at the entry-day close, and a live trader scanning that evening could only buy at the next open.
7. **Universe impurities.** The `CS` type includes some things that are not operating companies: SPAC shares, MLP units (e.g. MMLP, WLKP), some closed-end funds (e.g. CHY) and some preferred-like listings (e.g. BHFAL/BHFAM/BHFAN/BHFAO appear among the 2025 candidates). ADRs (type ADRC) are excluded. None of these change the conclusion, but the universe is not perfectly clean.
8. **The fee model is simplified.** A flat 3.5% per round trip ignores bid-ask spreads on sub-$5 names, which are often larger than 1% each way, and assumes IBKR tiered minimums. For the < $2M volume bucket, true costs are probably higher.
9. **Data problems met.** (a) The 403 history limit described above. (b) The 5 requests/minute limit, which made pagination slow. (c) The ticker `NA` was read as a missing value until I fixed the parser. (d) Holiday and weekend dates were avoided by picking trading days by hand. None of the chosen dates returned empty.

## 5. Verdict

**No. This evidence does not support a rule a small cash account should trade.**

- In the one full design year available (2025), the median trade in every variant **lost money after the 3.5% round-trip cost**. The median net return ranged from −1.58% (best: Dec 20 entry, price >= $5, entry $ vol >= $2M) to −9.66% (worst: low-liquidity names). Before fees, the losers mostly **lagged** the equal-weight universe over the same window instead of beating it.
- The only positive-looking result, the Dec 31 entry mean of +4.84%, comes from one +967% stock. Its median was −0.65% gross and −4.15% net.
- The 2024 proxy year agrees: the gross median is around 0% to +2% and every net median is negative.
- The illiquid-loser bucket, where the thesis says the effect should be strongest, was the worst in both entry variants.
- Your own kill criterion in `RULES.md` is "backtest median net of fees <= 0, or the result depends on a handful of huge winners". **Both conditions are met.**

Expected result per year if traded anyway: taking the most favourable variant (Dec 20 entry, price >= $5, entry $ vol >= $2M), the typical (median) trade in 2025 was **+1.92% gross, about −1.6% net**, with roughly half of trades losing money before fees. On 3 positions of about $60, that is roughly −$3 in a typical year. Individual trades ranged from about −18% (10th percentile) to about +24% (90th percentile).

What would change this verdict: a data plan with history back to at least 2015, so all 11 Decembers can be tested. The 2025 result could come from an unusual year. But the burden of proof is on the strategy, and at a $60 position size the 3.5% fee drag is larger than any median gain measured here. **Larger positions (so the $1 minimum becomes a small cost) would be a precondition for revisiting this, not a detail.**
