Bumble sells for $6.05 a share. The premium is 138%, and the price is 86% below the IPO.

Ithaca Holdings pays about five times the cash Bumble produced in the last 12 months, for an app whose paying users fell 16% in a year. The vote is already done, so there is very little left for a new buyer.

Decision: tracked, already run. Not a trade.

Rung 1

What is it?

On 12 Oct 2026 Bumble Inc. (BMBL) signed a merger agreement with Ithaca Holdco, Inc. Each Class A share is converted into $6.05 in cash. The release, issued at 08:24 New York time on 13 Oct, calls that a 138% premium to the 9 Oct close and values the company at $1.3 billion.

Rung 2

What does it actually mean?

The premium is large because the starting price was very low. At $2.54 the market valued the whole business, debt included, at about $747M. That was 2.8 times the cash it produced from operations in the last 12 months. The deal lifts that to about 5 times.

  • The user base is falling. Paying users were 3.16M in the second quarter against 3.78M a year earlier, down 16.4%. Revenue for the quarter fell 15%, to $210.5M.
  • The buyer's price in plain terms: about $1.34B for $893M of trailing revenue and $267M of trailing operating cash, which is 1.5 times revenue and 5 times cash (our arithmetic).
  • For anyone who held from the start, $6.05 is 86% below the $43.00 IPO price, and only 13% above the $5.34 close of a year ago.

What the filings do not say

  • No expected closing date. The only date is the 12 Jul 2027 deadline.
  • The "other approvals" by governments are not named in the 8-K.
  • The size of the Greenoaks commitment, and of any borrowing, is not disclosed.
  • How many shares the founder is rolling over is not in the 8-K.
  • There is no tax-receivable payment in the merger agreement. The June 10-Q says that agreement was ended in November 2025 by one-off settlement payments. We did not read the amount.
