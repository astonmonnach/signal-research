Elmet's lock-up is over. Most of the unlocked stock sits with insiders whose sales are capped

20.6M shares, about 68% of Elmet, came out of the IPO lock-up today. But no venture fund or buyout firm is waiting to sell. Two-thirds of those shares belong to the chief executive and other insiders, who can only sell in limited amounts and must report each sale. The big unknown is one co-founder with 5.1M shares. Meanwhile, the defence story that lifted the stock is not yet in reported revenue.

Rung 1What is it?

The company. The Elmet Group Co. (Nasdaq: ELMT, SEC CIK 2101698), based in Portland, Maine, makes tungsten and molybdenum wire, rod, powder and parts at plants in Lewiston (Maine), Coldwater (Michigan) and Euclid (Ohio). It also makes high-power microwave equipment. In the twelve months to 3 Jul 2026, revenue was $228.5M and adjusted EBITDA $31.8M (company figures, non-GAAP). Critical Materials Components was 86% of that revenue and Engineered Microwave Products 14%. By shipments it is mostly a molybdenum business: 59.4% molybdenum against 19.3% tungsten in Q2. Aerospace, defence and government customers were 39.3% of Q2 revenue, on programmes such as Hellfire and Javelin.

The IPO. Priced on 22 Apr at $14.00; the company sold every share, with no selling stockholders. The underwriters used their full over-allotment option on 23 Apr, so 9,857,142 shares were sold for about $138M gross when the deal closed on 24 Apr. Cantor Fitzgerald led it, with Needham, Canaccord Genuity and Roth.

The lock-up, in the filing's own words. Directors, officers and "holders representing 100% of our outstanding common stock" agreed not to sell "for a period of 180 days after the date of the underwriting agreement" (22 Apr). Day 180 is 19 Oct (my arithmetic). The company's own promise not to issue shares ran to the 180th day after the prospectus date, also 19 Oct. The "Shares Eligible for Future Sale" section gives a second date: "beginning 181 days after the consummation of this offering, up to 20,624,611 additional shares may become eligible for sale". Counting from the 24 Apr closing, that is Thu 22 Oct (my arithmetic). The prospectus also says: "Cantor may release all or some portion of the shares subject to lock-up agreements at any time and for any reason."

The Department of War deal (8-K, 14 Sep)

On 11 Sep Elmet signed an investment agreement with the Department of War (DoW). On 14 Sep the DoW paid $200M for 200,000 shares of Class A Redeemable Preferred Stock, part of up to $450M. The other $250M comes in tranches of at least $25M each, which can be drawn from six months after closing, subject to DoW conditions and "the availability of appropriations". The preferred accrues a 5.5% dividend paid in more preferred, ranks ahead of the common, and the holder can demand redemption after ten years. With it came a penny warrant for 5,675,506 shares at $0.001 and a strike warrant for 1,891,835 shares at $15.92: 7,567,341 shares, both exercisable from 14 Sep 2027. The DoW also gets a board seat, consent rights over new senior equity, affiliate deals and new debt, and a ban on common dividends and buybacks. Separately, the Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) stockpile office awarded tungsten contract SP8000-26-D-0029, effective 11 Sep. It has a "Minimum: $150,000,000.00 (guaranteed)" and a "Maximum: $2,000,000,000.00", and the contract states that the award "does not obligate funds".

Rung 2What does it actually mean?

The unlock is smaller in practice than the headline. For the next three months, an affiliate can sell the greater of 1% of the shares outstanding (304,595, my arithmetic) or the average weekly trading volume over the four calendar weeks before filing a Form 144. Yahoo volumes give about 2.8M to 3.0M shares a week depending on the window (my arithmetic). That is the binding cap, and it shrinks as trading slows: the 14 Sep deal day traded 14.02M shares and has already dropped out of the window. So Anania's 10.8M shares, aggregated with his trusts and company, can come out at about 2.8M per three months at most. The amount that can be sold without a cap is about 2.0M shares, from Lamare and the remainder, rising to about 7.1M if Schott is not treated as an affiliate (my arithmetic).

What the facts say about lock-up expiries

I checked 14 US IPOs from 2025-26 whose prospectuses state a plain 180-day lock-up. For each, I measured the five sessions around the first day holders were free to sell (close three sessions before that day to close two sessions after) against IWM. Result: the stock beat IWM in 8 cases and lagged in 6. The median was +2.9 points and the mean +5.2 (my arithmetic). There is no reliable drop. ELMT has already lagged IWM by 7.7 points over the ten sessions before its expiry; the sample's median for the same stretch was -0.6.

The published study (I read the abstract, not the paper). Field and Hanka, "The Expiration of IPO Share Lockups", Journal of Finance 56 (2001), studied 1,948 lockups. The abstract reports "a statistically prominent three-day abnormal return of -1.5 percent" and a 40% permanent rise in volume. It finds both much larger when the firm is venture-backed, and that venture capitalists "sell more aggressively than executives and other shareholders". Elmet has no venture backers.

Later DoW tranches bring preferred stock only; the October deck says the warrants were "issued at initial closing". The preferred compounds at 5.5% a year, quarterly. Left alone for ten years, $200M becomes about $345M (my arithmetic). If all $450M is drawn, the senior claim grows with it.

The sleeper point. The deck sells the penny warrant as "partial repayment of preferred balance". The certificate of designations sets a sliding scale: 0% of the penny warrants' value reduces the preferred while the 30-day average price is less than 100% above $15.92, then 45% to 65% at higher levels. Below about $31.84 (my arithmetic), the DoW keeps its full $200M senior claim and also gets the 5.68M penny-warrant shares. At today's price that is about $106M of stock on top of the preferred.

VerdictAVOID

Why AVOID stays: the supply is now real. Insiders are capped, but about 2.0M to 7.1M shares can be sold without a cap, and nobody knows what Schott will do. The stockpile story fails LT1: no stockpile revenue has been reported, the guaranteed $150M has no order, and deliveries wait for new supply (the Springer restart is targeted for 1H 2029). And the deal premium has not been given back: the stock is still +15.8% since 11 Sep while every critical-minerals peer is down about 23% or more.

What would move it to WATCH: (1) the price back near $15.92 to $16.19 without a marketed secondary; or (2) two weeks after 22 Oct with no Form 144 or Form 4 sales from Anania or Schott; plus (3) a Q3 10-Q that shows the $35.6M contract and growing defence backlog. What would move it toward OWN: a funded DLA delivery order under the $150M minimum (LT1), at a price that leaves room against the peers (LT4).
