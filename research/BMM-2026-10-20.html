Blue Moon's C$10 deal with Elmet: the "65% premium" is mostly a warrant and a falling share price

Elmet (ELMT) has paid C$35M for 3.5M Blue Moon units, and the money can only be spent on the Springer tungsten complex in Nevada. The price was fixed on 11 Sep, when it was 31.8% above the market. On my numbers, a share plus its three-year warrant was worth about C$10 that day. Springer has its state permits and a mill, but no current resource and no study. The US$25M prepayment that was meant to close alongside the shares has not been announced.

Rung 1What is it?

The closing. On 19 Oct Blue Moon Metals (TSXV: MOON, Nasdaq: BMM, SEC CIK 1732379) said it had closed a non-brokered placement with The Elmet Group: 3,500,000 units at C$10.00, C$35,000,000 gross. Each unit is one share and one warrant, exercisable for three years at "$10.80". The 19 Oct release does not name the currency. The 14 Sep release and Elmet's 8-K both say C$10.80 (CAD 10.80), subject to TSX Venture Exchange approval. The net proceeds "will be used solely to fund the development of the Springer Project", and Elmet's 8-K gives it audit rights over that. The shares and warrants carry a Canadian hold of four months and one day, and the placement still needs final TSXV approval. No finder's fee was paid, and no new insider or control person was created.

  • Board seat: under an investor rights agreement, William Jacob Homiller joined Blue Moon's board from 16 Oct. He is a sitting Elmet director (Elmet proxy, 16 Sep: "W. Jacob Homiller, Director", 89,469 shares). Elmet keeps the seat while it holds at least 40% of the equity it bought. It also gets participation, top-up and information rights.
  • Elmet's stake: about 3.1% of the roughly 111.2M shares the company counts after this deal, or about 6.1% if the warrants were exercised (my arithmetic; the company's own September deck shows "Elmet, 3%").
  • Part of a bigger package (14 Sep, signed 11 Sep): Elmet plans about US$150M around Springer. US$25M is this equity. US$50M is a tungsten prepayment facility in two US$25M tranches, repaid through a 25% credit on Springer concentrate sales with no interest unless in default. US$75M goes into a joint venture that will own and run the ammonium paratungstate (APT) plant: Elmet 70%, Blue Moon 20%, EQ Resources (ASX: EQR) 10%. Elmet and EQ set aside a further US$25M for overruns. Blue Moon keeps 100% of the mine and mill. It contributes the APT plant through "an irrevocable and exclusive 99-year use and operating agreement", at a value still to be set.
  • Whose money: Blue Moon's release says that of the US$450M the Department of War (DoW) committed to Elmet on 14 Sep, "US$150 million has been designated as use of proceeds for the Transactions".
  • Going the other way: on 21 Sep Elmet issued Blue Moon a warrant for 1,166,970 Elmet shares at $21.423. It can be exercised from six months after issue (about 21 Mar 2027) and lasts three years. Blue Moon's deck calls the two warrants "reciprocal warrants totalling approximately US$25 million of strike value".

Rung 2What does it actually mean?

Why would Elmet pay 65% over the market? Mostly, it didn't choose to. The terms were fixed on 11 Sep, when C$10.00 was 31.8% above the C$7.59 close. Elmet's 8-K calls the subscription "a binding commitment". The 65% gap is what was left after MOON fell to C$6.05 before closing. C$10.00 is also exactly what public investors paid in May, when Blue Moon sold 10,625,000 shares at C$10.00 and placed 5,000,000 more at the same price.

My arithmetic: Black-Scholes, three years, a 3% interest rate, no dividends, and no discount for the four-month hold or for dilution. Volatility is my assumption: MOON's realised volatility was 76% over the last year and 68% over the last three months (Yahoo closes), and long-dated volatility is usually lower. In the 60% case the warrant is the whole premium on 11 Sep, and 39% of the C$3.95 headline gap today.

What the rest of the money buys Elmet. A seat on Blue Moon's board held by its own director. Control of the APT joint venture, which its deck calls the "first independent U.S. APT plant", with four of seven board seats. The right to take 100% of Springer's concentrate. And a source of feed for its Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) offtake: a guaranteed minimum of $150M of tungsten ore, concentrate and sodium tungstate over five years, with up to $1.85B more at the DLA's option. The DLA "shall have the right to determine the source", from "certain pre-determined sources". Elmet's filings do not say whether Springer is one of them. In return, Elmet gave Blue Moon a warrant on its own shares. I estimate that warrant at US$5.5M to US$10.8M at 40% to 80% volatility (my arithmetic; Elmet's realised volatility is about 99%).

The sleeper point: only the equity half has arrived. On 14 Sep Blue Moon said Elmet "is expected to close on a US$50 million investment in the BM Group" within 45 days, "half of which is the Equity Subscription and half of which is the Tranche 1 investment". Day 45 is Thu 29 Oct (my arithmetic). The 19 Oct release covers the shares only. US$50M also matches, to the dollar, Blue Moon's own internal estimate of what a Springer restart costs. Elmet's 8-K says the full set of Springer deals, including the JV and the prepayment, is expected to close "within the next twelve calendar months".

Tungsten: the supply squeeze is real, and both companies plan on the price halving

China. On 4 Feb 2025 China's Ministry of Commerce and customs authority (Announcement 2025 No. 10) put APT, tungsten oxide, some tungsten carbide, certain solid tungsten and tungsten alloys, and the related technology under export licensing. In May 2026 the ministry's export-control site published an enforcement case: a fine for 20 kg of tungsten powder smuggled out without a licence. The USGS (February 2026) says China produced 67,000 t of the world's 85,000 t of mined tungsten in 2025, about 79% (my arithmetic). It also says the US has had no commercial tungsten mine since 2015, and that Rotterdam APT rose from $331 to $675 per MTU during 2025.

US demand. From 1 Jan 2027, 10 U.S.C. 4872 bars the Defense Department from contracting for covered material "mined, refined, or separated" in China, Russia, North Korea or Iran. Covered material includes tungsten metal powder and tungsten heavy alloy, and germanium and gallium (relevant to Apex) are added from Dec 2027. The stockpile's FY2025 plan listed potential purchases of 2,041 t of tungsten (USGS); the FY2026 plan "was not released".

Read-across. The deal day faded. BMM opened 20.8% higher on 14 Sep and closed only 4.1% up, giving back 13.8% from open to close; it fell another 7.7% the next day. Since then MOON is 14.2 points behind IWM and 9.5 behind XME. But it is 4.1 points ahead of REMX and 14.4 ahead of the median tungsten peer, which fell 34.7% (my arithmetic). The 24% drop since the announcement is mostly the tungsten group selling off, not Blue Moon news. The closing release itself moved nothing.

Already run? It ran earlier: from C$3.73 a year ago to a C$11.59 peak close on 9 Apr, then half of that was given back. The Elmet news was priced and faded on 14 Sep. So there is no unpriced deal premium left to capture. What is left are dated events, mostly months away.

Takeable for a UK account? Yes, through the US listing. BMM trades on the Nasdaq Capital Market at $4.29, with a 20-session average of $1.44M a day (median $1.23M). That passes the $250k bar, but not the $5M long-term bar (LT5 flagged). MOON on the TSXV adds about C$2.1M a day for brokers that offer it. Blue Moon pays no dividend, so withholding tax does not arise. BMM tracks MOON closely: C$6.05 at 1.405 CAD per USD is $4.31, against $4.29 (my arithmetic).

VerdictTracked, no call

Why no call: the money is real and ring-fenced for Springer. But the news was priced and faded on 14 Sep, and the closing moved nothing. Since then BMM has fallen with the tungsten group, not because of anything in its filings. Most of the "65% premium" is a warrant plus a price that fell after the terms were set. What would move the value (the prepayment, a current resource, the JV papers, Nussir finance) is weeks to a year away, and each one could go either way. As a long-term name it fails LT1 (no revenue, no current resource at Springer) and LT3 (shares up 26% this year, with more to come), and LT5 is flagged (about $1.4M a day in the US).

What would move it to WATCH: the US$25M Tranche 1 announced by about 29 Oct, plus TSXV acceptance, while MOON keeps beating the tungsten peers. To AVOID: Tranche 1 missing its window with no explanation, a new equity raise priced below the market, or an adverse step on the Nussir permit. Toward OWN (long term): a current NI 43-101 resource at Springer, signed JV and prepayment agreements, and Nussir project finance closed, at a price that has not already run.
