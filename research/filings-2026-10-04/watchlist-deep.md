# Watchlist filings, deep read: VYLR/CTVA, THRM/MOD, TWST, MTUS

*Written 4 Oct 2026. Covers filings from 30 Sep to 2 Oct 2026, from `watch/digests/2026-10-03.md`.*
*Method: `nth-order-pipeline/METHOD.md`. Primary sources only. Prices are Yahoo daily closes through Fri 2 Oct 2026.*
*Anything marked **[UNVERIFIED]** comes from a non-primary source, or is my own inference. Don't log those items as evidence.*

---

## Five things that matter

1. **The forced seller in the Corteva spin-off is CTVA, not VYLR.** S&P put VYLR straight into the S&P 500 on 1 Oct. CTVA is "deleted" from the S&P 500 and moved to the MidCap 400. That takes effect **before the open on Tue 6 Oct, so the index trades happen at the close on Mon 5 Oct**. VYLR also moves from Materials to **Consumer Staples** on 6 Oct, so Materials funds sell it and Staples funds buy it. Source: the S&P DJI press release of 1 Oct.
2. **The value has already moved between the two halves.** CTVA + VYLR = 79.18 at the 2 Oct close. The last price that still carried the VYLR entitlement was 77.65 (30 Sep). So the sum is +2.0%, against +0.9% for SPY. Underneath that small gain, VYLR trades at about 20x 2025 pro forma EBITDA and CTVA at about 5–6x. Seed has re-rated up. Crop protection, which keeps the PFAS liabilities, has re-rated down. 167m CTVA shares changed hands on 1–2 Oct, which is 25% of shares outstanding.
3. **THRM's "dump" on 2 Oct was mostly the dividend going ex.** THRM fell from 33.63 to 31.30 (−6.9%). But the $2.07 special dividend went ex that same day. Adjusted for that, the fall was −0.8%, on 6.1m shares (14.5x normal daily volume). That still leaves about 17m of the 23.7m new shares that former MOD holders may not want. Note that MOD is in none of the S&P 1500 indexes, so S&P index funds are not forced sellers here.
4. **The seven TWST Form 144s are tax sell-to-cover, not real supply.** They total 101,246 shares (about $19.1m). That is 0.15% of shares and about 6% of one day's volume. The D&O lock-up from the 4 Aug offering at $96 ended on **3 Oct, so 5 Oct is the first trading day without it**. But insiders have already sold $99m under 10b5-1 plans that the lock-up allowed. The real overhang is legacy funds selling into a stock up 349% this year: ARK has gone from 10.35% to 5.11%, and William Blair from 8.6% to 3.5%.
5. **MTUS is confirmed and is not a signal.** It is a 10b5-1 plan adopted 20 May 2026, selling 100,000 RSU shares, by a CEO who retires on 31 Dec 2026.

**Litigation (#1, #2).** "On September 30, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit summarily reversed the District Court's order denying the State of California leave to file its motion for a temporary restraining and preliminary injunction order seeking to delay the previously announced separation… The Court of Appeals expressed no view on the merits… On remand, the District Court denied California's motion to enjoin the Separation."

The board also waived the closing condition on legal restraints: "Effective September 30, 2026, the board of directors of Corteva waived the Legal Restraints Condition to the extent such condition is not satisfied… solely due to the existence of a potential order, injunction or decree…"

The underlying claim is that the spin is a **fraudulent transfer.** Vylor's Information Statement (Ex. 99.1 to 8-K 0001193125-26-402928) puts it this way: "California, supported by 18 other states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and 9 political subdivisions, sought leave… to file a motion to enjoin the spin-off as a fraudulent transfer… If a court were to agree with such a plaintiff, then such court could void the spin-off or related transactions as a fraudulent transfer or impose substantial liabilities on us… the court could return to New Corteva or its creditors some of our assets or your shares of Vylor."

**Debt exchange (#3, #4).** Old EIDP notes were swapped for new Vylor notes:

| Series | Outstanding | Tendered → swapped into Vylor notes | % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.300% 2030 | $500m | $434.841m | 86.97% |
| 5.125% 2032 | $500m | $476.214m | 95.24% |
| 4.800% 2033 | $600m | $527.584m | 87.93% |

Holders who tendered early were paid about $2.67–2.90 in cash per $1,000. About $161m of EIDP notes stayed with New Corteva. Those stub notes have had "substantially all of the restrictive covenants and events of default" removed, according to the 20 Aug 8-K (0001193125-26-359264, Items 1.01 and 3.03). Holders of those stubs are now unprotected.

**Accounting (#7).** "Vylor has been treated as the 'accounting spinnor' and therefore is the 'accounting successor' to Corteva following the spin-off." In other words, the seed business is the larger, "surviving" company for accounting purposes. **As a result, VYLR's first 10-Q will carry Corteva's pre-spin history, and its reported numbers will be messy.**

**What these seven filings do not contain.** None of them has a new credit agreement, an index notice, a dividend declaration, or guidance. Those items sit in other primary sources:

- **Credit facilities** (Information Statement, "Description of Material Indebtedness"). On 6 Aug, Vylor signed:
  - a $3.0bn five-year revolving credit facility (RCF);
  - a $1.5bn 364-day RCF;
  - a $2.75bn delayed-draw term loan, intended as a backstop that "upon the completion of the spin-off, Vylor does not intend to draw";
  - a planned $3.5bn commercial paper programme.

  The pro forma assumes "full draw-down of the 364-Day Revolving Credit Facility, a $1.578 billion draw-down under the $3.0 billion Five-Year Revolving Credit Facility." The only covenant is "total indebtedness to total capitalization… not exceed 0.60." PHI, the seed subsidiary, stops being a co-borrower on the earlier of **31 Dec 2026** or repayment.
- **New notes** (8-K 31 Aug, CTVA 0001193125-26-377055). Vylor issued $550m of 5.125% notes due 2031 and $550m of 5.625% notes due 2036. Proceeds were "to make a cash distribution to EIDP." Separately, EIDP (New Corteva) issued $700m of 6.000% notes due 2036 (8-K 24 Sep, 0001193125-26-401156).
- **Cash moved from seed to crop protection.** "Approximately $3,078 million of cash has been distributed from PHI to EIDP… an additional $726 million to $776 million" was expected before the spin (Information Statement, capitalization footnote 2).
- **When-issued trading.** This was cancelled at the last minute. The 14 Sep release (Ex. 99.1, 0001193125-26-391369) had planned "VYLR WI" trading from 25 Sep. The 24 Sep release then said: "Corteva has determined that there will not be 'when-issued' trading in Vylor common stock or 'ex-distribution' trading in Corteva common stock prior to the distribution" (Ex. 99.1, 0001193125-26-401621). **So 1 Oct was the first day either stock was priced.**
- **Distribution ratio.** One VYLR share per CTVA share, record date 24 Sep. About 667,196,000 VYLR shares outstanding (Information Statement). New Corteva has 667.3m shares (CTVA 8-K #7, note i).
- **Dividend policy** (Information Statement): "We expect that we will pay a quarterly dividend following the consummation of the spin-off beginning in the fourth quarter of 2026… approximately 15% of annual cash flow from operations."
- **Guidance.**
  - Pre-spin Corteva guided FY2026 operating EBITDA of $4.1–4.3bn (8-K 30 Jul).
  - Vylor's own press release of 1 Oct (PRNewswire; not on EDGAR as of 3 Oct) sets 2029 targets of "$11.2 billion to $11.9 billion" net sales and "$3.3 billion to $3.7 billion" operating EBITDA. It also targets licensing income of over $500m in 2027.
- **Liability allocation** (Information Statement): "New Corteva will retain… obligations relating to historical PFAS and other environmental liabilities associated with legacy EIDP businesses, other than any such liabilities that are primarily related to the Seed Business."

### 1c. The forced-flow branch

**The index changes.** Source: S&P DJI press release, 1 Oct 2026. "Vylor Inc. (NYSE: VYLR) was added to the S&P 500 on October 1… Vylor replaces Corteva in the S&P 500. Corteva will replace Olin Corp (NYSE: OLN) in the S&P MidCap 400, and Olin will replace Qorvo, Inc (NASD: QRVO) in the S&P SmallCap 600 effective prior to the opening of trading on Tuesday, October 6… Vylor will have a Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS) change to Consumer Staples effective on October 6."

The same release also covers TWLO replacing WBD in the S&P 500, FORM moving into the MidCap 400, and WK moving into the SmallCap 600.

**Who is forced to sell VYLR? Almost nobody passive.**
- S&P 500 funds received VYLR in the distribution, and VYLR is now in their index. They have no trade to make.
- The real forced flow is the sector change. These are holdings from the SPDR fund files as of 1 Oct:
  - XLB (Materials) holds **4,310,898 VYLR (3.83% of the fund)** and must sell all of it at the 5 Oct close, about $290m.
  - XLP (Consumer Staples) holds none yet. To estimate its purchase I used ADM as a ruler. XLP owns 0.869% of ADM's shares; SPY owns 1.228%; the ratio is 0.707. Bunge gives the same ratio (0.709). Applied to SPY's 8,255,170 VYLR shares, XLP should **buy about 5.8m VYLR (~$390m, ~3.0% of XLP)**. That would make VYLR a larger XLP holding than ADM (2.52%).
  - **Net across SPDR's sector funds: about +1.5m shares bought.** **[ESTIMATE]**
  - MSCI-based sector funds (Vanguard, Fidelity) also follow GICS, but I could not verify when MSCI applies the change. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- Funds with mandates that don't fit VYLR. The Information Statement warns: "Vylor common stock may not be included in some indices in which Corteva common stock is included, causing certain holders to be mandated to sell." In practice this means chemicals and materials specialist funds. The low dividend (about 15% of operating cash flow) also suits income funds poorly.

**Who is forced to sell CTVA? S&P 500 and Materials funds, at the 5 Oct close.**
- SPY holds 8,171,794 CTVA. XLB holds 4,284,838 CTVA. CTVA leaves the S&P 500, and so the Materials Select Sector index, too.
- MidCap 400 funds buy. MDY plus SPMD own 1.27% of Olin's shares, which implies they need about 8.5m CTVA.
- **Net for SPDR funds only: about −4m shares (~$48m). [ESTIMATE]**
- I could not get the iShares or Vanguard holdings (the iShares site blocked the download), so the industry-wide net is unknown.
- Either way, the 167m shares that traded on 1–2 Oct are far larger than any of these index trades. Active holders and arbitrageurs had already done most of the selling before the index event.

**Spillover to other tickers (30 Sep to 2 Oct).**
- FMC $8.92 → $8.44 (−5.4%, on 2–3x normal volume). Bayer ADR −6.4%. MOS −2.7%. NTR −0.3%. Crop-protection peers were marked down alongside New Corteva.
- OLN +2.6%, on the move down to the SmallCap 600.

### 1e. What would change the view, and dates

- **5 Oct close / 6 Oct.** The index trades complete. If CTVA does not stabilise once the forced selling stops, the de-rating is fundamental (PFAS, crop-protection pricing; FMC at $8.44 shows how bad that sector is). If it does stabilise, the drop was mostly flow.
- **First earnings.** No date has been filed on EDGAR. Corteva has historically reported Q3 in early November. A secondary listing shows **4 Nov** for VYLR. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **First VYLR dividend.** Expected in Q4 2026. The size, measured against the ~15% of operating cash flow policy, will be a test.
- **Fraudulent-transfer case** (South Carolina district court, Fourth Circuit). No dates are scheduled. If the case revives, that is a tail risk for VYLR, because its assets or shares could be clawed back. If it is dismissed, that removes an overhang from both stocks.
- **31 Dec 2026.** PHI is released as co-borrower under Vylor's revolving credit facilities. This is minor.

## 2. THRM (Gentherm) / MOD (Modine): Reverse Morris Trust closed 1 Oct 2026

### 2a. What the 8-Ks say

Sources: THRM 0001193125-26-410776 (Items 1.01, 2.01, 2.03, 5.02, 5.03, 7.01, 9.01) and MOD 0001104659-26-112870 (Items 1.01, 2.01, 5.02, 7.01, 8.01, 9.01).

**Deal terms.**
- Each MOD shareholder received one share of the spun-off company per MOD share, and each of those became **0.44619 THRM shares.**
- "Gentherm issued 23,735,961 shares of Gentherm Common Stock to the Record Date Modine Shareholders… approximately 43.62%," with existing THRM holders keeping 56.38%.
- The cash payment to Modine was "$155,991,275… a reduction from the $210,000,000… as a result of the exchange ratio adjustment." MOD used it "to prepay outstanding borrowings."

**Special dividend.** "$63,500,492, or $2.07 per share… payable in cash on October 7, 2026, to Gentherm shareholders of record as of… September 28, 2026… Record Date Modine Shareholders… will not be entitled to the Cash Dividend." On 17 Sep the estimate had been $1.90 (MOD 8-K 0001104659-26-108614). The increase came because "trading… has decreased the overlapping ownership."

**New THRM debt.** A "$250.0 million… senior secured delayed draw term loan" with Bank of America, fully drawn at closing. The rate is SOFR + 1.125–2.000%, it is "secured by substantially all of the assets of SpinCo, Gentherm," and it carries minimum interest-coverage and maximum net-leverage covenants.

**Price paid.** "Based on the closing price of Gentherm common stock on September 30, 2026, the transaction valued the Performance Technologies business at approximately $946.4 million" (MOD Ex. 99.1).

**People and governance.**
- Paul Mascarenas (former Ford CTO) joins the THRM board, which goes to 10 members.
- THRM's authorised shares rise from 55m to 110m.
- Jeremy Patten left MOD for THRM. Katrin Schatz becomes interim president of Modine Performance Technologies.

**Brand.** THRM now owns the "Modine" brand. MOD licenses it back: exclusive for 4 years in heating, ventilation, air conditioning and refrigeration (HVAC&R), non-exclusive for 2 years in heat transfer.

**Pending filings.**
- MOD's pro forma financials are due "not later than four business days after the Closing Date," which is **7 Oct**.
- THRM's pro forma financials are due within 71 days, around **17 Dec**.

### 2b. Forced-flow branch: will holders dump THRM?

- **Size versus liquidity.** The 23.74m new shares equal **56 days** of THRM's average daily volume (421k shares over the past 3 months). Post-deal THRM has about 54.4m shares, worth about $1.70bn at $31.30.
- **2 Oct, the first day after the shares were issued.** Volume was 6.12m shares, 26% of the new shares.
  - The price fell from 33.63 to 31.30.
  - But Yahoo dates the **$2.07 dividend ex on 2 Oct**. Adjusted for that, THRM fell only −0.8%. The intraday low of 28.92 was −8.4% adjusted.
  - So the first wave of selling was absorbed by the close.
- **Index mechanics.**
  - The SPDR files for 1 Oct show **no MOD line in SPY, MDY, SPMD or SPSM. MOD is not in the S&P 1500**, so no S&P index fund received THRM and has to sell it.
  - THRM *is* in the S&P SmallCap 600: SPSM shows "GENTHERM INC WHEN ISSUED," 282,150 shares. If S&P raises THRM's index share count, SmallCap 600 funds become **buyers**. I could not verify when S&P applies that change. **[UNVERIFIED]**
  - In Russell indexes, MOD (about $9.5bn) is probably a large/mid-cap and THRM a small-cap. If so, funds tracking only the Russell 1000 would sell. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Mandate mismatch.** This is the real seller. Investors owned MOD for data-centre growth. What they received is a car, truck and off-highway parts maker: Yahoo lists THRM as "Auto Parts," and its release lists "light vehicle… commercial vehicle, off-highway."
- **What is already priced.** THRM fell from $45.61 (23 Jul) to $33.30 (30 Sep), −27%, before the deal closed. It is −13% since the deal was announced on 28 Jan, while MOD is +21%. Much of the expected selling was priced in advance.

### 2c. Does MOD (soon Modexus) change classification?

- **What the continuing business looks like.** MOD's first fiscal quarter of FY2027, the June quarter (8-K 0001104659-26-088230):
  - Data Centers: $348.6m (+90%).
  - Commercial HVAC: $261.6m (+22%), "primarily driven by higher coil sales to data center customers."
  - **Data Centers is therefore about 57% of the remaining segment sales.** It is not a pure play. MOD describes itself as "data center cooling, commercial HVAC and refrigeration."
- **Current labels are still automotive.** EDGAR shows SIC 3714 "Motor Vehicle Parts & Accessories," and Yahoo shows "Consumer Cyclical / Auto Parts." No S&P or MSCI announcement about MOD's classification has been found.
- **Two branches to watch. [INFERENCE]**
  1. A reclassification from autos to industrials. Auto-sector funds would sell and industrials funds would buy.
  2. **S&P index eligibility.** MOD has 53.1m shares × $178.20 = **$9.46bn**, more than CTVA, which was just put into the MidCap 400. Inclusion is at the index committee's discretion.
- **Guidance.** MOD's FY2027 outlook ("+20% to 35%" sales, "$650 to $680 million" adjusted EBITDA) still includes Performance Technologies. "Following the close… Modine will issue an updated outlook reflecting the continuing business." That should come with the next earnings report.
- **Name change.** A special meeting will be held "within three months following the closing" to rename the company Modexus Solutions. The ticker stays MOD.
