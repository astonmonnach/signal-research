Rung 1What is it?

Curaleaf Holdings (TSX: CURA, OTCQX: CURLF), a US multi-state cannabis operator, made an unsolicited take-over bid for all of Aurora Cannabis (Nasdaq and TSX: ACB) on 18 Aug 2026. The first terms were US$0.75 cash plus 0.3463 of a Curaleaf share, capped at US$5.00. On 5 Oct Curaleaf raised the bid, and on 6 Oct it filed the Notice of Variation: US$1.00 cash plus 0.4013 of a Curaleaf subordinate voting share per Aurora share, capped at US$6.00, open until 11:59 pm Mountain Time on Fri 4 Dec 2026. Aurora's board, on the unanimous advice of a special committee, rejected the revised bid on 14 Oct. Curaleaf hit back in a release on 19 Oct. Curaleaf owned no Aurora shares when it launched the bid.

How the price and the cap work

  • Below the cap, the share count is fixed. You get 0.4013 Curaleaf shares whatever Curaleaf trades at. There is no floor or collar. Aurora's adviser points out that "the Offer provides no corresponding adjustment, and no minimum value protection" if Curaleaf falls.
  • The cap only bites if Curaleaf rises a long way. If Curaleaf's 20-day VWAP on the TSX is above C$17.76 at expiry, or on the day all conditions are met if that is earlier, the share count shrinks so the total is US$6.00. C$17.76 assumes C$1 = US$0.7015. CURA closed at C$10.78 on 19 Oct, so it would need to rise about 65% (my arithmetic). For practical purposes the cap is irrelevant today.
  • Where "$5.00" came from. Curaleaf's 6 Oct release priced the offer using CURA's 2 Oct close of C$14.21 at US$0.7015, which is about US$9.97 a share. The same release puts Aurora's unaffected price at a 30-day VWAP of US$2.75 to 10 Aug, which makes the offer an 86% premium. The $2.75 is a VWAP, not a close. ACB closed at $2.89 on 10 Aug, and my VWAP from Yahoo daily bars comes to $2.76.
  • The cash is about US$67M in total and is paid in US dollars, from Curaleaf's cash on hand. There is no financing condition. Fractional Curaleaf shares are rounded down, with no payment for the fraction.
  • Aurora's Notice of Change describes the cap as "based on a formula related to Aurora's share price". The Notice of Variation ties it to Curaleaf's own 20-day VWAP. The bidder's document is the one that governs.

The conditions (Section 4 of the Offer, unchanged by the variation)

  • Statutory Minimum Condition, which cannot be waived: more than 50% of Aurora's shares must be tendered, excluding any held by Curaleaf or other non-independent holders. On the 65,744,811 shares Curaleaf counted at 31 Aug, that means more than about 32.9M shares (my arithmetic; the count has probably grown since through Aurora's share sales).
  • Minimum Deposit Condition, which can be waived: at least 66⅔% on a fully diluted basis.
  • Regulatory approvals on terms Curaleaf finds satisfactory. The circular names Competition Act (Canada) approval and German foreign direct investment approval from the economics ministry. Phase I there is two months from a complete filing, and a Phase II review can run to four. Curaleaf says it "cannot exclude" German merger-control filing and is "continuing to assess" filings in Europe, Australia, New Zealand, the Caribbean and the United States. No filing I read reports that any of these approvals has been received.
  • The rights plan must not affect the bid. The board has to waive or redeem it, a regulator or court has to set it aside, or the bid must not be found to fall outside "Permitted Bid" status.
  • Wide walk-away rights, each judged "in its reasonable judgment". They cover a material adverse effect, any legal action or new law, and any Aurora action that reduces the bid's value to Curaleaf. That last one includes issuing shares (Aurora's at-the-market programme is the live case) or agreeing any merger, arrangement or asset sale. Market disruption also counts, and so do "any material regulatory changes including any that could have a material affect on Curaleaf's ability to export cannabis internationally from the United States". Aurora's board notes that last item concerns Curaleaf's business, not Aurora's. Curaleaf has not changed any condition.
  • Timing: if the minimum is met and the conditions are met or waived at expiry, Curaleaf takes up the shares "immediately" and pays within three business days. It must then keep the offer open for at least 10 more US business days for late tenders.

Withdrawal rights

  • Tendered shares can be withdrawn at any time before Curaleaf takes them up. They can also be withdrawn if they are not paid for within three business days of take-up, and for 10 US business days after most notices of change or variation. The Permitted Bid terms in Section 6 repeat the any-time-before-take-up right.
  • The variation deleted one clause: the right to withdraw at any time after 17 Oct if the shares had not been taken up. Because the any-time-before-take-up right remains, the practical loss looks small (my reading). Your broker will set its own, earlier, withdrawal and tender cut-offs.

The US cannabis issues

  • About 86% of Curaleaf's revenue comes from cannabis in US states: medical and adult-use, in 15 states. The Attorney General's rescheduling order of 23 Apr 2026 moved state-licensed medical cannabis to Schedule III. Adult-use cannabis remains illegal under US federal law. A DEA hearing on adult-use ran from 29 Jun to 15 Jul 2026, and the judge's timing is "uncertain".
  • Aurora holders would swap a Nasdaq-listed share for one that is not on any US exchange. Curaleaf "does not intend" to apply to NYSE or Nasdaq and says holders "will likely have to trade such shares on the TSX". In June 2026 Curaleaf also removed the rule that converted Boris Jordan's multiple voting shares if it listed on a US exchange.
  • Aurora says the bid "does not address the approvals of, or advance notice to, U.S. state cannabis regulators" that a change in Curaleaf's ownership may need. The circular names only Canadian and German approvals.
  • Curaleaf carries a US$468.5M uncertain-tax-position liability, mainly its Section 280E position, which the IRS is examining (10-Q, 30 Jun 2026).
  • For US holders: the Curaleaf shares "have not been registered or otherwise qualified for offer and sale in certain U.S. states". In those states no offer is made except to exempt institutions.

Aurora's board and its rights plan

  • Recommendation: REJECT, do not tender (14 Oct, unanimous). A special committee of four independent directors (Singer, Kohli, Beauchamp and Uttamchandani) is exploring alternatives. These include "a possible transaction with one or more third parties or remaining independent". The board says it will give a further recommendation later.
  • Inadequacy opinion: Fort Capital found the revised offer inadequate on 14 Oct and valued it at US$4.74 using 9 Oct prices. Its own letter says some of its fees "may be contingent on the Company arriving at an alternative transaction, or circumstances where no transaction is completed at all". Curaleaf makes the same point.
  • Insiders: directors and officers own 408,620 shares and do not intend to tender. Aurora knows of no holder above 10%. If Curaleaf took control and terminated them, six officers would receive change-of-control payments totalling about C$24.3M (my sum of the Notice of Change table; C$10.48M for the CEO, Miguel Martin), plus about C$2.19M for directors' DSUs.
  • Rights plan: yes. It dates from 2018, was last renewed on 9 Aug 2024 and triggers at 20%. A Permitted Bid must stay open at least 105 days. On 1 Sep Aurora said the original bid, which expired at 5 pm on 1 Dec, was not a Permitted Bid. Curaleaf then moved expiry to 11:59 pm on 4 Dec. The 14 Oct Notice of Change does not mention Permitted Bid status, and it says the deficiencies Aurora raised were "sufficiently remedied". The board can waive the plan for one bid. A waiver then also covers any other bid made by circular before that bid expires.
  • Two regulator applications. Curaleaf asked the Alberta Securities Commission on 14 Sep to halt Aurora's at-the-market share sales, and asked for an expedited hearing. Neither company's filings report a hearing date or a ruling. Aurora's own application, which sought pro forma statements and a longer deposit period, was dropped once Curaleaf's variation supplied both (decided 6 Oct). No court proceedings are disclosed.

What a rival bidder could look like, from the filings only

  • What is disclosed: from 8 Sep, Fort Capital approached "a number of parties". Aurora has confidentiality and standstill agreements with "multiple parties", who have had confidential information and "numerous discussions". On 6 Oct the committee discussed "the anticipated timing of receipt of expressions of interest", but the timing is not published. No filing names any party. Aurora says "there can be no assurance that the process will result in a transaction". Staying independent is one of the options.
  • The bar to beat is $4.06, not $5. On Curaleaf's own accounting, that is below Aurora's identifiable net assets.
  • Two routes in the documents. First, a competing bid: the board can waive the rights plan, and a waiver then covers any other bid made by circular before that bid expires. Second, a negotiated deal. If Aurora agrees a merger, arrangement or asset sale, Curaleaf's condition (f)(xii) lets it walk. Curaleaf also says it would then shorten its own deposit period to the legal minimum (Section 5).
  • My inferences, not in the filings: standstill parties usually cannot launch a hostile bid of their own without Aurora's consent, but the terms are not disclosed. Any buyer of Aurora's German operations would probably face the same German investment review that Curaleaf describes.

For a UK holder

  • You might not receive shares. Section 7 says no Curaleaf shares will be delivered to a non-resident of Canada unless they "may be lawfully delivered to such person without further action by the Offeror". If they cannot be, the depositary sells them on the TSX and sends the net cash, after expenses, commissions and withholding. Cash is paid in US dollars.
  • Where CURA trades: the TSX in Canadian dollars (CURA), and OTCQX in US dollars (CURLF). Neither is a US exchange.
  • Your broker: neither company's filings mention the UK. Curaleaf sells adult-use cannabis, which is illegal under US federal law. Whether your UK platform will hold CURA, take it in through a corporate action, or sell it for you is a question for your broker. Check with your own broker before tendering, and ask for its cut-off, which will be earlier than 4 Dec.
  • Canadian tax (circular, Section 19): a non-resident is generally not taxed in Canada on the exchange. That holds if ACB is listed on the TSX and you, with anyone you do not deal with at arm's length, have not owned 25% or more in the past 60 months. Draft rules from July 2026 would widen that 25% test to count options and interests. Canadian residents are taxed on the cash plus the market value of the shares received; there is no rollover.
  • US tax (circular, Section 20): for non-US holders the exchange is "not expected to result in any U.S. federal income tax consequences". Afterwards, Curaleaf treats itself as a US company for US tax. Dividends on CURA would face 30% US withholding unless a treaty form such as the W-8BEN cuts it, and the Canadian section separately describes 25% Canadian withholding on dividends to non-residents. Gains on selling CURA are generally not taxed in the US. US holders face uncertain reorganisation treatment and a risk under the PFIC rules for passive foreign companies.
  • The circular does not discuss US estate tax for non-US holders. Because Curaleaf calls itself a US corporation for US tax, it is worth asking a UK adviser about (my inference). UK tax is separate and is not covered here or in the circular.
