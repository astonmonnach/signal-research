Third Point wants Strata sold. The filings back the overhead complaint, not a bargain price

Daniel Loeb's fund turned activist on 15 Oct with 5.8% of Strata Critical Medical (the former Blade) and asked for a sale. The company's own numbers support its complaint: in 2025 overhead was bigger than gross profit, and in Q2 the company paid more cash to cover tax on shares vesting to its staff than the business made in free cash flow. But at the multiple Strata itself paid for Keystone, the business is worth roughly today's price. Any takeover premium depends on a buyer cutting that overhead.

Rung 1What is it?

Third Point LLC and Daniel Loeb filed a Schedule 13D, accepted at 16:09 New York time on Thu 15 Oct, after the close (event date 13 Oct). They hold 5,126,511 shares, 5.8% of 88,466,806, bought for $24,452,374. They had held 5,000,000 shares as a passive 13G holder since 4 Aug 2025, days after the Joby deal was announced on 1 Aug. The only open-market buys in the last 60 days were 126,511 shares at $4.53-4.55 on 12 and 13 Oct; the August and September rows in the trading exhibit are matched sales and purchases at the same price between two Third Point funds.

  • The ask (Item 4): the stock is undervalued; the business is strong but "not of a scale to support its current overhead"; management's incentive options are not aligned with shareholder value; the board should explore strategic alternatives, "including the consideration of a potential sale", run by a reputable investment bank.
  • What it does not say: no letter, no board nominees, no price, no named buyer, and no threat of a proxy contest. It does not say which "options" it means.
  • Company response: none. No 8-K or other company filing on EDGAR since the 13D, and the newest release on the investor site is dated 8 Sep (both checked 19 Oct evening).

What the business is now

  • The sale: on 29 Aug 2025 Blade sold its passenger business to Joby Aviation for 5,325,585 Joby shares, valued at about $75.4M, which it sold for $70.2M net. Joby may also pay up to $35M of earn-outs (a 12-month financial target and an 18-month staff-retention target), in cash or Joby shares at Joby's choice, plus up to $10M of indemnity holdbacks. The company carried these at $40.8M at 30 Jun, all due within a year.
  • What is left: organ transplant logistics and clinical services. Two segments: Logistics (air and ground transport of donor organs and medical teams, plus organ placement; Q2 revenue $48.2M, +6.9%) and Clinical (surgical organ recovery, normothermic regional perfusion, and perfusion staffing for heart surgery; Q2 revenue $24.3M). Clinical came from buying Keystone Perfusion on 16 Sep 2025 for $124M, which the company called about 9 times Keystone's 2025 adjusted EBITDA.
  • Customers: transplant centres, organ procurement organisations (OPOs) and hospitals, who pay Strata directly. No customer was 10% or more of revenue in Q2 2026. The air network is mostly third-party aircraft: about 35 aircraft at end-Q1, 10 of them owned. 601 employees at end-2025, 19 of them at headquarters.
  • Numbers: Q2 revenue $72.5M (+60.7%), adjusted EBITDA $7.9M, net loss from continuing operations $10.5M. 2026 guidance was raised on 4 Aug to revenue of $285-295M and adjusted EBITDA of $33-35M ($36-38M pro forma for this year's acquisitions).

Why cash fell from $58.7M to $16.3M in Q2

Acquisitions, not losses or debt. The Q2 cash-flow statement shows:

  • -$34.8M for three bolt-on deals (net of cash acquired): Ohio Valley Perfusion (30 Apr), Louisville Perfusion Services (2 Jun) and Heart and Lung Transplant National Recovery Program (22 Jun). Total price $42.4M: $35.2M cash, 742,060 new shares and up to $4.08M of earn-out. The company says it paid "mid-single-digit" multiples.
  • -$6.3M moved into US Treasuries, which still count as liquidity ($22.8M of cash and Treasuries at 30 Jun, against $58.8M at 31 Mar).
  • -$4.2M paid to cover tax on staff and executive shares that vested (net share settlement).
  • -$2.8M of capital spending, offset by +$5.7M of operating cash flow.
  • No borrowing. A $30M asset-based credit line with JPMorgan (signed 30 Jan 2026, to 2029, expandable to $50M) was undrawn at 30 Jun; the company says it is there to fund more acquisitions. Total liabilities were $53.4M against equity of $279.3M. The 14.2M SPAC warrants ($11.50 strike) expired worthless on 7 May 2026, so that overhang is gone.

The pay and "options" Third Point objects to (2026 proxy, 10-Q)

  • Two chief executives since Aug 2025: Will Heyburn (also CFO) and Melissa Tomkiel (also General Counsel), each on a $550,000 salary. 2025 total pay: $5.33M and $5.61M. The five named executives together: $19.3M in 2025 (my sum of the Summary Compensation Table, mostly stock valued at grant), more than the company's whole 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $14.1M.
  • The chairman works for the buyer. Founder Rob Wiesenthal became non-executive chairman and now runs Blade inside Joby. He gets a $3.75M transaction bonus ($3.0M paid monthly over 36 months), and 2,950,219 of his old Strata share awards were converted to vest on the passenger business's results and his staying at Joby. Strata carries them as a $16.9M liability. His 2,428,702 options have an $0.18 strike.
  • Options and share awards outstanding at 30 Jun: 2,584,248 fully vested options at a $0.19 average strike (no new options granted under the current plan) and 12,258,111 unvested share units and performance units, 13.9% of the share count (my arithmetic). Stock-based pay was $8.7M in H1 2026 and $17.8M in 2025.
  • Shareholders already pushed back: say-on-pay got 62.5% support in 2025. After changes (2026 performance units now measured over three years on adjusted EBITDA, with a relative-return modifier of up to 20%), it got 82.7% at the 28 May 2026 meeting (my arithmetic from the 8-K vote counts). Note that adjusted EBITDA adds back stock pay, so the target excludes the cost of the reward (my observation).
  • In a sale: cash severance is double-trigger, but unvested performance units vest at target if a buyer does not assume them (2026 proxy).

Rung 2What does it actually mean?

The overhead complaint is in the numbers. In 2025, SG&A (overhead) was $60.9M against gross profit of $41.1M. In H1 2026 it was $29.6M against $29.3M. The adjusted EBITDA the company guides to is positive only because stock-based pay and one-off costs are added back.

The sleeper point: in Q2 Strata paid $4.2M in cash to cover tax on shares vesting to its people, more than the $2.9M of free cash flow the whole business made. The Form 4s show who: about $3.8M of shares were withheld from four officers in Q2, and another 726,132 shares (about $3.7M at the withholding prices) from five officers in Q3 (my arithmetic from the Form 4s). The co-CEOs alone received 1,083,714 (Heyburn) and 1,224,004 (Tomkiel) shares from performance units in 2026, before shares withheld for tax (my sums). This is the cash cost of the pay structure Third Point is attacking, and it will show up again in the Q3 cash-flow statement (inference).

A clue the Joby money is coming. On 24 Sep the board certified the co-CEOs' "Earnout PSUs", which the proxy ties to the sold passenger business's results in its first 12 months, at 177,858 shares each: exactly 1.5 times the 118,572 target (my arithmetic). That suggests the passenger business beat the target that also drives the Joby earn-out (inference). The company has not announced any Joby payment. If the $40.8M carried at 30 Jun arrives, it is more than the $22.8M of cash and Treasuries the company holds now, with no borrowing (some of it may come as Joby shares).

Already run? No. The stock is 4.8% below Third Point's average cost, 27% below its 3-month high close of $6.24 (21 Aug), and down 12.9% over a year while SPY rose 14.2%. Liquidity is fine for this account: the 20-day median dollar volume is $3.82M.

How much power Third Point has. 5.8% cannot force a sale. The board is staggered, so only one class is up each year; the 2026 proxy lists the 2027 class as co-CEO Tomkiel and chairman Wiesenthal. Nominations for the 2027 meeting must arrive between 28 Jan and 27 Feb 2027 under the bylaws. Third Point's leverage is persuasion, other holders (about 31% sits with five names), and the threat of that window.

Rung 4What could happen next?

Base: talks, no process yetThe board engages privately and uses the Q3 results to defend the roll-up. The stock holds near Third Point's cost. Anchor: Campbell Soup (2018). Third Point's 13D said the only acceptable outcome was a sale, then it nominated a full slate; on 26 Nov 2018 it settled for two board seats, and no sale followed. Strata's equivalent pressure point is the 28 Jan to 27 Feb 2027 nomination window.
Bull: the board hires a bankA formal review leads to a sale. Anchors: Sunterra (13D demanding a sale, Jul 2006; $16.00 cash agreed Mar 2007, a 35% premium), Pogo Producing (Third Point got two board seats in Mar 2007; agreed sale to Plains Exploration in Jul 2007) and Soho House (13D Jan 2025; $9.00 cash agreed Aug 2025, 83% over the unaffected price; completed 29 Jan 2026). Caveat from Rung 2: at Keystone's multiple the value is about today's price, so a premium needs a buyer that removes the overhead.
Bear: the board says no and keeps buyingThe roll-up continues on the credit line or new shares, and Third Point either sells (visible in a 13D/A) or waits for the window, when only one class of seats is up. The reference price is the $4.28 close before the filing. Anchor: Cano Health (13D Mar 2022 asking for a review focused on a sale; no merger agreement was filed; Chapter 11 in Feb 2024). Cano was heavily indebted and Strata has no debt, so the lesson is that a board can ignore the call, not that Strata faces the same end.

VerdictWATCH

Why not more: no process exists, the board has not replied, and on the company's own acquisition multiple the stock is not cheap. The case for a premium rests on a buyer stripping out overhead, which only happens if the board agrees to sell.

Why not less: the complaint is real and specific, the stock has not run (-12.9% in a year, +4.6% on the news), there is no debt, and up to $40.8M from Joby may be on its way.

What would change it: towards OWN, a board statement opening a review or hiring a bank, Third Point adding shares, or a second holder going active. Towards AVOID, the board rejecting the call and doing more deals, any share issue, or Third Point selling.
