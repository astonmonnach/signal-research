## 1. MSGS: the Rangers spin-off, effective 26 Oct

- **The filing** ([8-K, 9 Oct](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2132873/000119312526419170/d122415dex991.htm), the information statement):
  - Holders of MSG Sports on the **record date, 20 Oct**, get **1 share of MSG Rangers Corp for every 2 MSG Sports shares**. The spin-off becomes effective on **26 Oct**.
  - The Rangers company lists on the NYSE as **MSGR**. MSG Sports keeps the Knicks, renames itself MSG Knickerbockers Corp and changes its ticker to **MSGK**.
  - About 9.84M Class A and 2.26M Class B Rangers shares will be issued (12.1M in total). When-issued trading is expected before the 26th; normal trading starts the next trading day.
- **What the Rangers company looks like** (same document):
  - Revenue of **$337.3M** in the year to June 2026, with an **operating loss of $30.5M** (a $23.3M loss the year before, a $26.8M profit the year before that).
  - Local TV money is shrinking. MSG Networks cut its rights fee by 18% from January 2025 after its own debt restructuring, and the deal now ends after the 2028-29 season. Local media was $35.3M, 10% of revenue.
  - Debt is light: a $250M credit line, undrawn at 30 June, plus $16.5M owed to the NHL.
  - The Dolan family will hold about **63.4% of the votes**, so it's a controlled company. Nobody can force a sale.
- **Sum of the parts:**
  - MSG Sports has 24.2M shares (twice the Rangers Class A count, plus the Class B). At $402.30 that's a market value of **$9.74bn**. Long-term debt was $242M and cash $164.5M at 30 June ([XBRL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001636519.json)), so the enterprise value is about **$9.82bn**.
  - Third-party valuations: the **Knicks at $9.75bn** (Forbes, November 2025, [reported here](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415934/knicks-sponsorship-up-28-team-valuation-now-10b.html)) and the **Rangers at $3.65bn** (Sportico, [reported here](https://bluelinestation.com/new-york-rangers-value-jumps-to-3-65-billion-2nd-highest-in-nhl)) or $3.8bn (CNBC).
  - That's $13.4bn to $13.55bn, so the shares trade about **27% below** those valuations. Put another way, the market is paying roughly the Knicks valuation and nothing for the Rangers.
  - Per share (my arithmetic, **an inference, not a filed number**): the Knicks valuation is about $403 per MSGK share and the Rangers valuation about **$302 per MSGR share**, or $151 per current MSG Sports share.
- **Why the gap may not close:** it isn't new. A 2021 article already called the stock "buying the Knicks and getting the Rangers for free". The valuations are magazine estimates of what a buyer might pay for control, and control isn't for sale. The Rangers business also loses money at the operating line.
- **Reaction:** over the last two weeks MSG Sports is **-3.4%** (from $416.34 on 28 Sep), against SPY +1.7% and IWM -0.4%. No pop on the 9 Oct date announcement ($407.17 to $402.30).
- **What's missing:**
  - I haven't measured the move on the day the spin-off was first announced.
  - No when-issued prices yet, so there is no market price for either half.
  - I haven't read the agreements between the two companies (arena licence, shared services), which decide how the costs are split.
- **Verdict:** **WATCH** (spin-off list). The dates are hard and the test is clean: small spin-offs often get sold by holders who only wanted the parent. The thing to watch is where MSGR trades in its first weeks against the ~$302 valuation marker. What would move it to OWN: MSGR sold down hard on forced selling with no bad news of its own.

## 2. PCRX: Viatris buys Pacira for $36.50 cash

- **The filing** ([Pacira 8-K, 8 Oct](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1396814/000110465926114571/tm2627240d2_8k.htm); [Viatris 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792044/000114036126039024/ef20083514_8k.htm)): a signed merger agreement dated 8 Oct. Viatris will start a **cash tender offer at $36.50** within 15 business days (so by about 30 Oct).
- **Conditions:** more than 50% of the shares tendered, and the HSR antitrust waiting period ending. Pacira's board approved it unanimously. Pacira pays a **$62.0M break fee** if it takes a better offer.
- **Reaction:** Pacira went from $25.20 to $36.39 on the day (**+44.4%**) on 28.9M shares, against SPY -0.4% and IWM -0.1%. It closed Friday at $36.31. Viatris, the buyer, fell 0.3% on the day (down 3.4% at the low) and is +0.9% since.
- **Spread:** $0.19, or **0.52%**. If the deal broke and the stock went back to $25.20, that's a 31% loss, so the price implies only about a **2% chance of a break** (my arithmetic).
- **What's missing:** I haven't read the full merger agreement (the end date and what Viatris must do if regulators object), or the investor presentation.
- **Verdict:** tracked, no call. The spread is far below the 3.5% fee hurdle.

## 3. SYNA: onsemi buys Synaptics for $123 cash

- **The filing** ([PREM14A, 8 Oct](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/817720/000114036126039142/0001140361-26-039142-index.html)): the preliminary merger proxy. Holders get **$123.00 cash** per share under an "Amended and Restated" merger agreement dated 1 Oct, with ON Semiconductor.
- **Spread:** Synaptics closed at $119.82, so the gap is $3.18, or **2.65%**. That's the widest of the new deals. Before the news it was $101.40 (30 Sep), so a break would cost about 15%. The price implies roughly a **15% chance of a break** (my arithmetic).
- **Why the gap is wider:** not established. Chip mergers usually need several countries' approvals and can take a long time, but I haven't read the proxy's conditions section, so that's an **inference**.
- **What's missing:** the conditions, the regulatory approvals needed, the end date, the meeting date, and why the agreement was amended and restated.
- **Verdict:** tracked, no call. It's below the 3.5% hurdle. It's the one to read properly next, because a spread near 3% with months to run is where the hurdle could be crossed.
