Catsimatidis takes 1stDibs to 7.8%, buying after the company stopped

John Catsimatidis's United Refining bought 449,500 more 1stDibs shares on 15 Oct at $4.07, about 69% of the day's volume. Rung 1What is it?

On Mon 19 Oct, after the US close, John Catsimatidis's group filed Amendment No. 1 to its Schedule 13D on 1stdibs.com (Nasdaq: DIBS), the online marketplace for vintage and luxury furniture, art and jewellery. The shares sit with United Refining, a Pennsylvania oil refiner owned through Red Apple Group. It bought 449,500 shares in the open market on Thu 15 Oct at a weighted average of $4.07 including commissions. It now holds 2,635,385 shares, 7.8% of the 33,615,293 outstanding at 31 Jul. They cost about $13,216,097 in total, an average of $5.01 (my arithmetic).

  • Size of the buy: 449,500 shares was about 69% of the 654,100 traded on 15 Oct (Yahoo volume; my arithmetic). DIBS closed at $4.275 that day, up 8.2%.
  • Filed on the deadline: two business days after the trade. Counting from the first 13D, the group had added 763,350 shares, 2.3% of the company (my arithmetic). That is past the 1% change that forces an amendment.
  • What changed in the filing: only Item 3 (money spent) and Item 5 (holding, trades). Item 4, the purpose, is not amended. It still reads as it did in Nov 2025: shares bought "solely for investment purposes", strong support for management and the board, and "no present intention to seek control".
  • A correction to the brief: the new filing says nothing about the buyback. The "$12 million share repurchase program" is cited in the Nov 2025 13D. That programme was finished by 30 Jun 2026, and a second $10M programme authorised in May was used up in May.
  • Funding: working capital. The filing notes that United Refining "regularly invests in securities" from its cash flow.

What the filings show about Catsimatidis as an investor

  • Turning Point Brands (NYSE: TPB). TPB's 8-K of 22 Mar 2024 called Red Apple "a long-term Turning Point shareholder" and nominated John Catsimatidis Jr. to its board. He filed a Form 3 as a director in May 2024. His May 2026 Form 4 shows United Refining holding 400,000 TPB shares. The pattern is a long-held stake that leads to a board seat. At DIBS no seat has been asked for in any filing.
  • Gristedes Foods, 2004. A going-private merger at $0.87 a share in cash by a company controlled by Catsimatidis, who was already Gristedes' chairman and chief executive (Schedule 13E-3, 10 Sep 2004). He has taken a company private before, but it was one he already ran.
  • United Refining Energy Corp, 2007 to 2009. A blank-cheque company sponsored by United Refining. Its deal to merge with Chaparral Energy failed the shareholder vote and was ended on 11 Dec 2009 (8-K).
  • Premier Alliance Group (later root9B), 2012 to 2015. Catsimatidis Sr. joined the board in July 2012 and received director options through Dec 2015 (8-K, Form 4s).
  • What is absent: EDGAR full-text search finds no other Schedule 13D by these filers, apart from United Refining's 2009 13D on its own blank-cheque company. DIBS is the only 13D on file for the Catsimatidis Jr. filer account. I found no activist campaigns in the filings.

Rung 2What does it actually mean?

A supportive holder is buying on dips, below his own average cost. It is not a control move. The business case his first filing made is half-delivered. Adjusted EBITDA has turned slightly positive, but the company still loses money under normal accounting (GAAP), sales growth is low single digits, and the buyback he cited has been spent.

Any sign of a take-private or strategic interest? Not in the filings

  • The Catsimatidis Item 4 says there is "no present intention to seek control", and the 13D/A leaves it unchanged. It backs management's plan, including "evaluation of current and future inorganic growth opportunities", which means 1stDibs buying things, not being bought.
  • DIBS's filing list since 2024 has no merger proxy, no tender offer, no going-private schedule (13E-3) and no merger agreement. The only material-agreement 8-K was the Insight share buyback in June 2024.
  • "Strategic alternative expenses" were $0 in 2025, $77k in 2024 and $1.06M in 2023 (10-K FY2025). The company's definition covers capital-return work as well as mergers, so even the 2023 figure does not prove a sale process.
  • Defences: the board is staggered into three classes (10-K risk factors). Any bidder would face a register where about half the shares sit with five holders, one of whom has just restated his support for management.

Rung 4What could happen next?

Base: a patient holder, and a stock that trades on its resultsCatsimatidis keeps the stake and adds now and then below the 1% amendment line, as he did between Nov 2025 and Aug 2026. The price then moves on Q3 results. Each of the last four results days moved it hard: +15.5% (7 Nov 2025), -14.1% (27 Feb 2026), -4.0% (8 May 2026) and +12.8% (5 Aug 2026), against IBUY moves of +2.7%, +0.1%, -2.2% and -0.8%. Anchor: Q3 guidance of GMV $89M to $94M and an adjusted EBITDA margin of -1% to 2%.
Bull: the company starts buying again, or a block finds a homeThe board has replaced each spent buyback so far. A new authorisation with the Q3 release would put the company's bid back next to his. A Benchmark block sold to a long-term holder, as Insight's was sold to the company at $5.2021 in June 2024, would remove the biggest overhang. Anchor: the 8-Ks of Aug 2024, Nov 2025 and May 2026, and the June 2024 Insight agreement.
Bear: no buyback, flat growth, thin tradingWith no buyback, GMV at the low end of guidance, and stock-based pay still around 13% to 16% of revenue, the stock repeats its June to October drift. Anchor: -18.8% from 29 May to 12 Oct against IBUY +2.6%. The low close in that slide was $3.67.
Takeover: possible, but nothing in the filings points to itCatsimatidis has taken a company private before (Gristedes, 2004), but he already controlled it. Here Item 4 rules out a control plan for now, there is no strategic review, and the board is staggered. It would take an amended Item 4 to make this a live scenario. Anchor: the Nov 2025 13D, unchanged on 19 Oct 2026.

Next results: Q3 2026. The date is not yet announced in any release or filing I found. Last year's Q3 came on 7 Nov 2025, with the date announced on 14 Oct. DIBS is a non-accelerated filer, so the 10-Q is due within 45 days, by Mon 16 Nov 2026 (my arithmetic).

Signals to watch:

  • An 8-K with a new buyback authorisation, most likely alongside the Q3 release.
  • Any 13D/A from United Refining, especially one that amends Item 4 or reports a sale.
  • A Benchmark, Beck Mack or Kanen 13G/A showing a lower stake. That would identify who has been selling the blocks.
  • Q3 GMV against the $89M to $94M range, and whether operating cash flow turns positive once the $5.9M reclassification is excluded.
  • Today's open: how much of the filing the price already reflects after the 15 Oct move.

VerdictWATCH

Why WATCH and not more: this is a supportive holder adding below his average cost. It is not a takeover signal, and the stock is not a cash box: price is about 2.1 times cash per share. The company's buyback, which absorbed blocks in the first half of 2026, has stopped. From the 14 Oct close, before the buy, the stock is up 7.6%, about 5 points ahead of the online-retail ETF. Some of that came on a day when the whole group rallied.

What would make the case stronger: a new buyback authorisation, Q3 GMV at or above $94M with positive operating cash flow, or a 13D/A that changes Item 4 or adds a board seat. What would make it AVOID: Q3 GMV below $89M or adjusted EBITDA margin below -1%, any sale by United Refining, or a large holder selling into the market rather than to a buyer. Review on the Q3 release, due by 16 Nov.
