Borr Drilling: the founder keeps buying, but the interest bill is what the shares are watching

Tor Olav Troim has bought 12.85M Borr shares since 13 August for $57.1M, and Monday's $4.44 close sits right on his average price. The fall since 13 October mostly hands back the jump his own buying caused. The bigger problem is the company's earnings: second-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of $43.8M was below the roughly $51M a quarter its new debt costs in interest.

Rung 1What is it?

Borr Drilling (NYSE: BORR) owns 29 jack-ups, the drilling rigs that stand on the seabed in shallow water, plus five more through a 50/50 joint venture in Mexico. Its founder and director, Tor Olav Troim, has bought shares on twelve days since 13 August through Drew Holdings, a company owned by a trust of which he is the beneficiary. Every Form 4 is code P, none ticks the pre-arranged plan (10b5-1) box, and the 13D calls the purchases from August to 9 October "open market".

  • Why a 13D now: the filing says it was made under 15 U.S.C. 78m(d)(6)(B) because Drew bought more than 2% of the company in twelve months. That rule takes away the passive-holder short form (13G). It is a paperwork trigger, not a declaration of activism.
  • Item 4 (intent): shares bought "for investment or other purposes". The holders "have no plans or proposals" for any Item 4 action, such as a merger, a board change or a sale. The only extra lines are standard: he may buy or sell more, hedge, or "distribute the Common Shares ... to other entities", and as a director he "may participate in discussions" on governance and operations. No board talks, no merger, no take-private. That absence is a finding.

The footnote says it "was effected in connection with a cashless exercise of vested stock options" granted in 2021 at $1.66. The officers' 2 and 3 Sep sales were tax withholding on vesting shares, as their footnotes say.
  • Selling on the other side: on 9 June, Granular Capital, the fund of director Thiago Mordehachvili, sold 8,000,000 shares at $4.70. Troim bought 1,063,000 the same day at the same $4.70. Granular still held 38,199,677 shares afterwards, about 12.4% (my arithmetic).

Rung 2What does it actually mean?

Insider buying this large usually reads as "the founder thinks it is cheap". The filings support a narrower reading. He is buying a company whose earnings fell for four quarters in a row, whose new work is priced below last year's, and whose debt costs more than it earned in the second quarter. The share price is being set by those numbers, not by his buying.

Why is it falling while he buys? Seven things, from the filings

  1. It is handing back its own spike. From 2 to 9 October, when he bought 6.75M shares and was 16% to 20% of each day's volume, BORR rose 22.3% while the other drillers rose 4% to 7%. Since 9 October it has fallen 7.1% while they rose 4% to 7%. Net, from 2 October to Monday, BORR is +13.6% against RIG +13.9%, SDRL +10.0% and NE +8.2%. His daily size shrank from 2.5M shares (9 Oct) to 0.5M (15 Oct), and no Form 4 has been filed for any later day yet. My inference: the marginal buyer stepped back and the extra gain went with him.
  2. Earnings are at a low, and the debt costs more than the second quarter earned. Adjusted EBITDA went $135.6M, $105.2M, $88.5M, $43.8M over the four quarters to June. The company blamed the Odin's delayed start in the US ($22.5M of costs in Q2), six rigs between contracts, Middle East insurance and fuel costs, and $10.8M of bad debts from a former West African customer. It expects Q3 to "improve significantly", with about 23 rigs working against 21.2, but gave no number. See the chart below.
  3. Dayrates for new work are lower. Contracted 2025 work averaged $145k a day. New 2026 commitments averaged $125k (5 by February), $120k (13 by May) and $123k (21 by August, $541M of backlog). That is about 15% below 2025 (my arithmetic). 2026 coverage slipped from $137k to $134k a day.
  4. The war that lifted oil is hurting jack-up work. Borr has four rigs in the Arabian Gulf. In March three were stood down and the Arabia III was shut in after an incident on a customer's platform; all had restarted or were restarting by mid-April. In Q2 fuel cost $5.1M more (partly rigs moving between jobs) and insurance $2.2M more because of the conflict. The chief executive said on 11 Aug that the conflict "has reduced near-term visibility, delaying tenders, contract start dates". Valaris took a $30M hit to Q2 EBITDA, mainly war-risk insurance for its Gulf jack-ups. Brent rose 50% from $72.92 (30 Jun) to $109.39 (19 Oct); BORR rose 7.5% in the same period.
  5. Its peers rose on deepwater news. Transocean has 27 floaters and no jack-ups. It announced a $1.1B backlog update (9 Oct), Brazil's competition authority cleared its takeover of Valaris (13 Oct), a bigger credit line (15 Oct) and $550M of US Gulf drillship awards (19 Oct). Valaris trades at 15.235 Transocean shares, so the two move together. "RIG was flat" held from 13 to 16 Oct (-0.5%), but RIG then rose 4.8% on Monday's awards. The peer gap is partly deepwater against shallow water.
  6. Share supply is large. Borr sold 71M new shares in 2025: 30M and 20M at $2.05 (July and August) and 21M at $4.00 (December). Shares outstanding rose 26% from the end of 2024 to 30 June 2026 (my arithmetic). Granular sold 8M in June and still holds about 38.2M. Borr's 20-F names Granular, Azvalor and Drew as large holders whose sales "may adversely affect" the price. No new shares were sold in 2026: there is no prospectus supplement on EDGAR since Dec 2025.
  7. It is not a refinancing scare. The June refinancing pushed almost everything out. Nothing is due in 2026, $50.9M in 2027 and $146.0M in 2028. At 30 June there was $473.6M of liquidity ($223.6M cash plus an undrawn $250M credit line). The risk is the ratio of earnings to interest, not a maturity.

The sleeper point: in the second quarter Borr's Adjusted EBITDA covered only about 86% of a quarter's coupon on its new debt (my arithmetic). From July 2027 the notes also start paying down at about $26M a quarter, so the bar to clear goes to about $77M. Q1's $88.5M cleared it and 2025's average ($117.5M) cleared it easily. Q2 did not. Troim's $57M is about 3.4 months of that interest bill. The Q3 number decides this, not his buying.

How big is the buying?

  • Against volume: 3.8% of the 336.2M shares traded from 13 Aug to 19 Oct (47 sessions), 10.9% on the twelve days he bought, and 19.5% at the peak (5 Oct). The whole 12.85M is 1.5 days of the current 20-day average (8.74M shares, $39.0M a day). All my arithmetic from Yahoo volume.
  • Against the float: Drew (40.1M) and Granular (38.2M) hold 25.5% between them. His 12.85M is 5.6% of the other 229.1M shares. Two passive funds also file as 5% holders: Cobas (31.3M at 13 Aug) and Azvalor (28.6M at Nov 2025). Excluding all four, 169.1M shares are left, and he bought 7.6% of that. The four together hold 45.0%. All my arithmetic; the Cobas and Azvalor counts are as old as their last filings.
  • His cost against today's price: $4.4435 average since 13 Aug against the $4.44 close, 0.1% below. Including his March, April and June 2026 buys (14.91M shares), the average is $4.525 and today's price is 1.9% below it. The March ($5.1958), April ($5.5818) and June ($4.70) lots are 14.5%, 20.5% and 5.5% under water. His 13 Aug lot ($4.0245) is 10.3% up.

On its weak first half, Borr is the most expensive name here apart from Valaris, which is priced through the takeover, and it is by far the most indebted. If its EBITDA gets back to 2025's level, its 7.8x would be in line with the others (7.1x to 9.5x). So today's price already assumes a recovery. It is not cheap on current numbers. On assets, the market values Borr at about $127M of EV per owned rig. Its balance sheet carries the rigs at about $105M each. VerdictWATCH

Not already run, not cheap. BORR trails its peers by 29 to 67 points this year, and its October spike has gone. But at 13.9x first-half EBITDA it is priced for a recovery that Q3 has to deliver. The founder's buying is real, large and on the record. It is not a floor: his March to June lots are under water, and the 13D announces no plan beyond holding.

What would change it. Toward OWN: Q3 Adjusted EBITDA at or above about $77M a quarter, new work at or above $134k a day, and no new shares. Toward AVOID: Q3 below about $51M (earnings not covering interest), an equity raise, or Granular selling again. Review on the Q3 results day. As a long-term holding it already fails gate LT3: shares outstanding rose 26% in 18 months.

Method checks: primary filings read (12 Form 4s, the 13D and 13D/A, the 20-F, the Q2 report and release, releases since January); "already run" checked against peers; reaction measured against IWM, OIH and four peers, close-to-close; input price Brent and WTI; siblings (Valaris's Gulf jack-ups) and contagion (Transocean's deepwater news, Elliott cutting Seadrill to 4.9% on 8 Oct) checked.
