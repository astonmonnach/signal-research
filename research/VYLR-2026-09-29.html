Corteva splits in two on 1 Oct. Vylor gets the growth, Corteva keeps the PFAS.

The seed business leaves with 66% of the profit, about $5.7B of debt and none of the legacy DuPont PFAS liabilities. The first weeks of trading are a price-discovery fight, not a buy signal.

Decision: WATCH, not a day-one trade

Rung 1

What is it?

Corteva (CTVA) is spinning off its seed business as Vylor Inc. (NYSE: VYLR). Holders of record at the close on 24 Sep 2026 get 1 VYLR share per CTVA share, distributed before the open on 1 Oct 2026, when regular-way trading begins. There is no when-issued trading, so the market sees VYLR's price for the first time on day one. After the spin, Corteva keeps no stake in Vylor.

Rung 2

What does it actually mean?

Corteva is separating a faster-growing, higher-margin business from a slower one. Seed is 57% of sales but 66% of segment EBITDA, and seed EBITDA grew 19% in 2025 against 6% for crop protection.

The liabilities are what separate the two companies. The separation agreement says New Corteva "will retain" obligations for "historical PFAS and other environmental liabilities associated with legacy EIDP businesses", except any that are primarily seed-related. That puts the 2021 Chemours/DuPont PFAS MOU and settlements like the North Carolina deal on 9 Sep 2026 on the Corteva side. On that deal, Corteva's share is ~$66M and it guarantees 29% of Chemours' share. Vylor takes a fixed 62% (the "Applicable Percentage") of only the narrower buckets: disclosure liabilities from the spin, shared spin costs, and orphan liabilities not on the schedules.

The balance sheet Vylor starts with:

  • Pro forma at 30 Jun 2026: $5,659M of borrowings against $1,100M of cash, which is roughly 2.1× 2025 seed EBITDA (our arithmetic, not a company figure).
  • $3,078M is on floating-rate revolving credit at an assumed 4.67%, and a commercial paper programme of up to $3.5B is planned.
  • Seed is a seasonal cash business, and the filing warns that spin-date balances "will differ" from the June pro forma.

Vylor also expects about $166M of separation costs.

Signals to watch:

  • 1 Oct: VYLR's first print, and the combined VYLR + CTVA value against CTVA's 30 Sep close.
  • Any S&P Dow Jones Indices notice on how VYLR is treated. Before the spin that decides whether index funds are forced sellers.
  • Final results of the EIDP-for-Vylor note exchange, and the actual spin-date debt against the $5.7B guide.
  • First dividend declaration and the first standalone quarterly report.

What the filings don't say

  • No index decision found. We searched S&P DJI for a VYLR announcement and found nothing. Whether index funds are forced to sell is still open.
  • No dividend amount. The filing only says Vylor "expect[s]" to pay a quarterly dividend.
  • No explanation for CTVA's September slide. CTVA fell from $89.97 (2 Sep) to $77.87 (29 Sep), −13%. None of Corteva's 8-Ks from 10–25 Sep explains it: they cover the NC PFAS settlement, the Form 10 amendment and the record-date notice. We don't attribute a cause.
  • No standalone VYLR valuation. Nothing traded before the spin, so any price target now is a guess.

Already run?

VYLR can't have run because it hasn't traded yet. CTVA, the parent, is +15% over a year ($67.63 → $77.87) but −13% since 2 Sep. The edge, if any, comes after day one. The plan is to let the forced and indifferent selling show itself, then look for a base after the index decision and the first results.
