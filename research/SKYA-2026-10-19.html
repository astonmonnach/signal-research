SkyAI's $3.00 tender: a real offer, but not a gap for a UK holder

SkyAI sold all its Solana and is buying back up to 95% of itself at $3.00 in cash. The offer is funded and the stock trades at $2.79. But the company will treat the payment as a dividend, so US tax is withheld from non-US holders, and for a UK account the "gap" mostly disappears.

Rung 1What is it?

On 16 Oct SkyAI (formerly Sharps Technology, Nasdaq: SKYA) filed a tender offer to buy up to 95% of each class of its securities at $3.00 cash: 48,311,489 shares and 18,775,877 warrants, up to $201.26M. On 15 Oct it sold all its SOL and held about $223.8M of cash. The company says $3.00 is about 0.95 times its net asset value (70,618,282 shares and warrants against $223.8M). The offer expires one minute after 11:59 pm New York on Thu 29 Oct "unless extended", and a release on 19 Oct repeated that date.

  • Proration: pro rata within each class if more than 95% is tendered. There is no odd-lot priority for holders of under 100 shares.
  • Minimum: 50% of each class (the two pre-funded warrant classes count together). No financing condition.
  • Conditions, each judged "in our reasonable judgment": material adverse change, legal action, a competing proposal, any new holder buying more than 5%, and a delisting risk that the company decides itself.
  • No SOL condition. An earlier release (9 Oct) made the offer depend on SOL staying above $100. The filed offer has no such condition: the SOL is gone.
  • Tax: "the Company intends to treat such payment as a dividend distribution for withholding purposes". Non-US holders face withholding at 30% of the gross unless they file an IRS W-8 form claiming a lower treaty rate.
  • Insiders: none of the directors or officers has said whether they will tender. A shareholder rights plan (adopted May 2026) is still in place.
  • The fight around it: Bastion Trading (Wei Zhu, about 8.6%) filed a 13D on 3 Sep citing payments to companies controlled by a director's brother, the unratified rights plan and the rushed annual meeting. At the 18 Sep meeting all five directors were re-elected under plurality voting despite withhold votes of roughly two to three times the votes for. On 14 Oct Bastion said in a wire release that it wants at least $3.50, will not tender unless its demands are met, and may go to court. Forward Industries made two all-stock approaches (June, then 0.306 FWDI per share in September); both were rejected.

Rung 2What does it actually mean?

This is a crypto-treasury company giving up. It raised money to hold SOL, the shares fell far below the value of what it held (about 0.57 times NAV at the 9 Oct close of $1.82, by the company's own NAV), and now it is handing back nearly everything at close to NAV. The market repriced it in one jump: the first trade after the weekend announcement was $2.335, against $1.82 on Friday 9 Oct.

The timing problem. US rules say an issuer tender "shall remain open" for at least twenty business days from commencement (Rule 13e-4(f)(1)(i)). Counting from 16 Oct, 29 Oct is only the tenth. Unless the company has relief it has not disclosed, the expiry has to move to about 12 or 13 Nov (my arithmetic, skipping the 11 Nov federal holiday). That delays the cash by two weeks.

Rung 4What could happen next?

Base: extended, then completedThe company files an amendment moving the expiry to about 12-13 Nov to meet the 20-business-day rule, buys up to 95%, and pays from cash it already holds. The price drifts toward $2.95-3.00 for US holders as the date nears. Anchor: Rule 13e-4(f)(1)(i) and the $223.8M of cash against a $201.26M maximum.
Bear: the company walksEvery condition is judged by the company. A competing proposal, a new 5% buyer, or its own view that the offer risks a delisting would let it amend or end the offer. The reference point is the $1.82 close before the news, about 35% below today. Anchor: Section 7 of the Offer to Purchase.
Bear, version two: Bastion blocks or delays itBastion says it will not tender below $3.50 and may sue. If its pre-funded warrants sit in the pre-funded class, they are about 28.6% of the 14.82M sought there (my arithmetic), which makes that class's 50% minimum harder. Shares and warrants are separate classes, so this hits the warrant leg first. Anchor: Bastion's 14 Oct release and the class-by-class minimum in the offer.
Bull: someone pushes for moreThe price is 0.95 times NAV, and Bastion is asking for $3.50. A bump would need the board to pay above its own asset value, which it has no reason to do unless the offer is failing. Anchor: Bastion's demand; Forward's two rejected approaches.

What happened to the others who handed cash back (no other treasury company has run a fixed-price tender for its common shares, so these are open-market buybacks):

  • ETHZilla sold about $40M of ether in Oct 2025 to buy back shares at roughly 0.72x its asset value. Four weeks later the shares were down 50%, against ether down 28%.
  • FG Nexus sold ether to buy back about 46% of its shares, then pivoted into affordable housing involving a company linked to its chief executive. It fell 24% on the pivot day and is down 64% since Oct 2025.
  • Empery Digital bought back 22M shares mostly with debt; an activist won board seats in Oct 2026. Down 64% against bitcoin down 31%.

The lesson for SKYA: the people who take the cash do fine. The people left holding the shell afterwards have not.

Signals to watch:

  • An amended Schedule TO (SC TO-I/A) moving the expiry or changing a condition.
  • Any director or officer saying they will or won't tender.
  • A 13D/A from Bastion Trading, or a new filing from Forward Industries.
  • The preliminary result, due the business day after expiry.
  • Your broker's corporate-action notice: its cut-off will be earlier than the company's.

VerdictTracked, no call

Already run: the stock gapped from $1.82 to a $2.335 open on 12 Oct, the first trading day after the announcement, and closed at $2.60 that day. What is left is a 7.5% gross gap that needs about four weeks and a company that keeps the final say.

For this account: tendering is worse than selling at today's price unless the withheld tax can be reclaimed. A long position is a bet that the market price climbs toward $3.00 as US funds buy in, with roughly 35% downside if the offer is pulled.
