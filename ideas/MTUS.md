## The trigger
On 25 Sep 2026 (after the close), the DLA awarded Metallus a **sole-source, firm-fixed-price contract worth up to $995M** for High Fragmentation 1 steel (artillery shells). It runs 5 years to 24 Sep 2031 with no option periods.
- Against a ~$780M market cap that's **127% at the maximum**, or about **25% of market cap per year**.
- The market barely reacted: it **opened +3.5% on 28 Sep, then gave it back**. Price on 1 Oct: ~$19.25.

## Why the market might be wrong (the translation)
- **Sole-source over 5 years** means the DoD is locking in Metallus as the supplier of a critical shell steel, while artillery demand is high.
- **The balance sheet can carry it** (SEC XBRL, 10-Q Jun 2026):
  - $108.6M cash and no debt;
  - H1 2026 revenue $649M and net income $14.3M;
  - buying back shares ($7.9M in Q2).

## What could make this wrong
- **$995M is a ceiling, not guaranteed orders.** Actual orders could be much smaller.
- **It may be a renewal** of supply Metallus already provides. If so, it adds little new revenue, which would explain why the stock didn't move.
- **H1 2026 operating cash flow was negative (−$14.1M).**
- Steel is cyclical: auto/industrial demand and prices matter more than defence.

## Renewal check (4 Oct 2026, after entry)
**Verdict: a new buyer for an existing product. Not a renewal, but not new revenue either until orders are placed.**

1. **Who is buying.** The buyer is the **DLA for the National Defense Stockpile**. The steel goes into storage in Columbus, OH, delivered within 24 months of each order.
   - Solicitation SP800026R0016 was posted **2 Jun 2026**. It was sole-source under 10 U.S.C. 3204(a)(3)(A), the industrial-mobilization exception.
   - HigherGov describes it as new, not a renewal. We found no earlier comparable DLA HF-1 contract.
   - Today Metallus sells HF-1 into the Army's shell supply chain. A stockpile buyer is a second channel.
2. **It's an IDIQ with no stated minimum.** DLA "will place orders as needed".
   - The maximum is **438.48M lb** over 5 years (≈219k short tons). Year 1 is up to **104.44M lb (≈52k tons)**.
   - That's roughly equal to **all of Metallus' 2025 aerospace & defense shipments (55.9k tons)**, and 8% of total 2025 tons (631.7k).
   - The $995M ceiling divided by the maximum tons is ≈$4,540/ton, against a realized A&D price of $3,005/ton in Q2 2026. The dollar ceiling has headroom.
3. **Defence is already material (10-K FY2025, 10-Q Q2 2026):**
   - A&D sales were $115.0M (2023, 8%), then $134.9M (2024, 12%), then $161.4M (2025, 14%).
   - H1 2026 A&D sales were **$112.0M against $74.6M (+50%)**. Q2 2026 alone was $60.1M on 20.0k tons.
   - Metallus has made HF-1 continuously since 1993 (army.mil, 2 Sep 2026).
   - $99M of Army-funded HF-1 capacity was commissioned on 26 Aug 2026.
4. **Other producers.** We found no other named domestic HF-1 producer. The sole-source basis is "keep this supplier available for mobilization", and the Army funded capacity at Metallus specifically. That supports a moat, but it isn't proof that no other source exists.
5. **Why the muted reaction?** The requirement was public from 2 June, so the award may have been expected. And with no minimum, the market can't count any of it yet.

**What Q3 (6 Nov) must show:** whether DLA has placed a first delivery order (size and timing), A&D tons and price, and how stockpile volume fits alongside Army-chain demand on the new capacity.
